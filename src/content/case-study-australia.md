One of two regional studies. The shape is set out on
[the case studies page](/case-studies): a HONEY cycle answering _how would the
requirements for NECTAR change here?_

Australia is the more recent arrival of the two: an industry that had to build
a response to the mite under time pressure, which makes the constraints unusually
visible.

## Hear: the situation on the ground

Establishment, spread and reinvasion pressure, described as the people working
through it describe it rather than as a summary of the literature.

## Observe: practice, cost, regulation, attitudes

What is currently done, what it costs, what the regulator requires, and how
beekeepers feel about a genetically engineered input. Four different questions;
keep them separate.

## Navigate: what an acceptable treatment would need to do

Duration, labour, cost ceiling, and regulatory classification. Stated as
requirements, because that is what they became.

## Evaluate: what this means for NECTAR

Where our design already meets those requirements and where it does not. The
places it does not are the useful part of this page.

## Yield: what changed, and what is still open

The design consequences, linked to the pages that carry them. Then the questions
this region raised that we have not answered.

## The regulatory reading

Why an inactivated product sits differently from a live engineered organism in
this jurisdiction, and what that does and does not settle. Detail on
[safety and security](/safety-and-security).
