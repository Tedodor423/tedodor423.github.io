The question this page answers is "what would happen after iGEM?", answered
with numbers where we have them and marked clearly where we do not.

## Lead with the chain

Most entrepreneurship pages start with a product and work toward a price. Ours
runs the other way, and that is the most interesting thing about it:

**What a beekeeper can afford → allowable cost per dose → allowable
manufacturing cost → required production titre.**

An affordability constraint, taken from stakeholder evidence, becomes a
specification for the wet lab. Open with that. Detail on
[economic modelling](/economic-modelling).

## The product

What the user physically receives, how it is stored, and what they do with it.
Concrete. If this cannot be described in three sentences, it is not yet a
product.

## The user

Who buys it. Commercial and small-scale beekeepers are different customers with
different constraints, and pretending otherwise weakens the case.

## User journey

Order → store → apply → replace → dispose. Each step is somewhere the product
can fail commercially, independent of whether the biology works.

## Current alternatives

What is used today, what it costs, and why it is not sufficient. Fair to the
alternatives: an unfair comparison is visible immediately and discredits the
rest.

## Unmet needs

Grounded in what stakeholders told us, not asserted. Each need traceable to the
conversation it came from.

## Value proposition

Quantified where possible. "Cheaper and more sustainable" is not a value
proposition; a stated cost against a stated alternative is.

## Manufacturing

The production route at scale: fermentation, downstream processing, drying and
formulation. Marked 🔵, modelled, not built.

## Techno-economic analysis

Cost structure and, critically, its sensitivity to production titre. Show the
range rather than a single figure, because the key input is currently unmeasured
and pretending otherwise would misrepresent the confidence.

## What we would need that we do not have

Skills, facilities, partners and capital. Naming the gaps is more credible than
implying a student team could do all of it.

## Beyond the first product

If NECTAR is a platform, Varroa is the first market rather than the only one.
The deck names six adjacent applications:

- Bee colony health
- Salmon and sea lice
- Shrimp aquaculture
- Mosquito control
- Crop fungal disease
- Biopesticides and agrotech more broadly

Keep this short and clearly **Proposed**. It shows the platform argument has
somewhere to go without pretending any of it is underway, and a judge reading
an unhedged market list will discount the page around it.

Where a genuinely good idea is out of scope for the time available, it belongs
here too as a productisation path rather than as work done.
