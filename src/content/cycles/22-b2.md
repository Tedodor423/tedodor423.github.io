# Iteration 2 — delivering a known dose to adults: the PER assay

**Question.** Cage feeding gives a cage average at best, even with a smaller feeder
([cycle B1](/engineering#cycle-b1)). Can we feed each bee a known volume instead,
reliably enough to state a dose per animal?

**Design.** We tested a system that would let us feed honeybees exact volumes of
known-concentration dsRNA solution, controlling uptake bee by bee. On the advice of
our bee lab PI, bees are restrained in proboscis-extension-response (PER) harnesses
with head and thorax protruding, unharmed, and fed from the tip of a 19-gauge
syringe. The harness is usually a mortality-testing tool; we wanted it as a dosing
instrument, so no response scoring, sucrose-response-threshold series or olfactory
conditioning was done. Behavioural PER work belongs in [future work](/future). After
feeding, haemolymph is taken by the centrifugal method of
[cycle B3](/engineering#cycle-b3).

**Build, in two rounds.**

1. **The pilot.** We first harnessed adult forager bees collected at hive entrances
   and fed each 5 µL of 1 M sucrose from the syringe.
2. **2.5 µL increments.** We repeated the setup with newly emerged bees, each
   receiving **2 × 2.5 µL** rather than one 5 µL drop, to reach the same 5 µL total:
   at 200 ng/µL, a **1 µg dose per bee**. Up to 18 bees were fed at once (five
   repeats for each of three dsRNA lengths, plus three controls), with feeding start
   times staggered **20 minutes** apart between length groups so that short time
   points stayed accurate.

**Test.** Round 1: whether caught workers would take the droplet at all. Round 2:
run as the dosing protocol for the full adult-haemolymph stability assay, described
on [the bee lab](/bee-lab) and in
[experiments and protocols](/bee-lab-experiments).

**Result: cohort selection, 14–15 July** `[CALC]`.

| Cohort        | n   | Observation window   | Survival        | Uptake     |
| ------------- | --- | -------------------- | --------------- | ---------- |
| Newly emerged | 40  | **overnight**        | **38/40 (95%)** | 5–10 µL    |
| Foragers      | 50  | **same working day** | **22/50 (44%)** | 10–30 µL ‡ |

‡ Uptake volumes were measured by graduated tip. The forager range needs
cross-referencing to the original record: the assay was run by a second operator and
the bench note gives an upper figure only, so the lower bound is provisional.

The pilot failed as a dosing method. Many droplets were dropped or absorbed onto the
bees' bodies, and many of the workers escaped. **The two windows are not matched, and
the asymmetry disfavours foragers further**: newly emerged bees were scored after a
full night, foragers within the same day. Forager mortality was front-loaded, with
about a quarter of the 50 lost within the first hour of restraint, which points to
handling and restraint stress rather than a slow decline. Fifty animals were
harnessed in about two hours, a throughput compatible with a multi-timepoint design.
Every newly emerged bee readily took the 5 µL dose, and haemolymph extraction
afterwards yielded 8–15 µL per bee ([cycle B3](/engineering#cycle-b3)).

**What we learnt.** A larger dose delivered to an animal that dies before its
timepoint is not a larger dose, so volume was **the wrong quantity to maximise**.
Splitting the drop in two is what made the volume accurate: a single 5 µL droplet is
large enough to roll off or soak into the bee. The bee you choose is a design
variable, not a supply constraint. Newly emerged bees, fed in two 2.5 µL increments,
gave the most consistent intake of a known volume and concentration, and because
they are within 2–3 days of maturing into nurse bees, testing them also mirrors the
stage of the bee's development we are interested in.

**Individual dosing uses fewer animals, and the reason is statistical rather than
ethical.** For a two-sample comparison at α = 0.05 and 80% power, the ratio of
animals required by the cage route against the individual route is
R = (CV_cage / CV_individual)² × DE, where DE = 1 + (m − 1)ρ is the design effect
from clustering, ρ the fraction of variance attributable to the cage rather than the
bee, and m the bees sampled per cage per timepoint. R is independent of effect size,
arm count and timepoint count, which is why it is quoted rather than a single bee
total.

Individual dosing carries measurement variance only: the column-extraction CV of
**18.5% (n = 7)**, combined with an assumed biological response CV of 30%, gives
CV = 0.352. Cage feeding adds dose variance on top; treating the reported 8.8-fold
intra-cage spread as a log-normal 95% interval gives CV_dose = 0.60 `[CALC]`.

| CV_dose  | CV_cage   | Variance ratio | _R_ at ρ=0.1 | ρ=0.3     | ρ=0.5 |
| -------- | --------- | -------------- | ------------ | --------- | ----- |
| 0.40     | 0.533     | 2.29           | 5.5×         | 11.9×     | 18.3× |
| **0.60** | **0.696** | **3.90**       | 9.4×         | **20.3×** | 31.2× |
| 0.80     | 0.874     | 6.15           | 14.8×        | 32.0×     | 49.2× |

Worked against our own design, 3 arms × 4 timepoints, 50% difference, 80% power:

| Route                           | Replication                                | Animals   | dsRNA required            |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | --------- | ------------------------- |
| Individual dosing               | 8 bees/group × 12 groups, +5% harness loss | **101**   | 101 µg                    |
| Cage, 15 sampled/cage/timepoint | DE = 5.2 → 132 cages                       | **1,980** | 190 mg (**2,112× stock**) |
| Cage, 3 sampled/cage/timepoint  | DE = 1.6 → 51 cages                        | **765**   | 73 mg (**816× stock**)    |

**8–20× fewer animals, and the two constraints fight each other.** The clustering
penalty is reduced by spreading bees across more cages with fewer samples from each,
but dsRNA scales with _cages_, not bees. The statistically better cage design (51
cages) is the one that consumes more dsRNA relative to what it delivers. No
configuration of the cage experiment is both powered and affordable. **Reduction,
refinement and replacement are served by the same change that makes the measurement
valid.**

**Limitation.** The staggering interval is recorded two ways in our own write-up: 20
minutes in the assay method and in the list of experimental difficulties, 15 minutes
in this cycle's own table. The wiki uses 20 throughout.

**What it changed.** Newly emerged bees and PER feeding became standard for every
adult dosing experiment on this project, which is what makes a µg-per-bee figure mean
anything. **Delivered dose: 5 µL × 200 ng/µL = 1 µg per bee**, now a known dose
rather than one inferred through averages. This setup was used for the full adult
haemolymph stability assay.
