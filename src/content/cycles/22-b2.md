# Second DBTL — redesigning the feeder so the dsRNA is affordable

**Question.** [Cycle B1](/engineering#cycle-b1) ended on two numbers: 74% of the
weight loss was evaporation, and 96.9% of the dsRNA loaded was never drunk. Can a
feeder hold a volume commensurate with what a cage actually drinks — about
250 µL/day rather than 8 mL — without changing what the animal consumes?

**Design.** Testing different 3D-printed inserts for sucrose feeding tubes in the
cage feeding assay, to minimise spillage and increase the accuracy of honeybee
consumption rates. Replacing 2 ml tubes with PCR tubes reduces the volume of dsRNA we
need to produce. Adapters were produced to seat a 0.2 mL PCR tube in place of a 2 mL
feeding tube, in **three geometries: horizontal, tilted at 15°, and vertical**. Two
feeding ports were made per tube with sterile hypodermic needles, **one near the base
and one at the neck**, the upper port being required to break the airlock that
otherwise prevents flow as the reservoir drains. Port diameter was set with
hypodermic needles at **20, 21 and 23 gauge** (0.90, 0.82 and 0.64 mm outer
diameter).

**Build.** The horizontal geometry was rejected on handling before quantitative
testing began: surface tension at the lower port could not be maintained against
contact, any touch to the underside of the tube caused immediate discharge, and
spillage occurred during routine transport of cage trays. The remaining two
geometries were run as a **2 geometries × 3 gauges × 3 replicates** factorial, 18
cages of 15 bees (270 animals) plus **five bee-free evaporation controls**, weighed
over two days. The original perforated 2 ml Eppendorf was kept as the comparison.

**Test.** Tubes were filled to the brim with 1 M sucrose, weighed, and inserted into
the mini-cages. After 24 h in the incubator, they were re-weighed on the same
balance.

**Result** `[CALC]`:

| 15° tilted      | 20G   | 0.367            | 0.290 | 0.0193    | **15.7**   |
| 15° tilted      | 21G   | 0.395            | 0.318 | 0.0212    | **17.2**   |
| 15° tilted      | 23G   | 0.430            | 0.353 | 0.0236    | **19.2**   |
| Vertical        | 20G   | 0.540            | 0.438 | 0.0292    | 23.7 ⚠     |
| Vertical        | 21G   | 0.549            | 0.447 | 0.0298    | 24.2 ⚠     |
| Vertical        | 23G   | 0.327            | 0.224 | 0.0149    | 12.2 ⚠     |
| _2 mL benchmark_ | —     | _1.092_          | _0.305_ | _0.0205_ | _16.7_     |

⚠ Box 17 was annotated as unusable at the bench and is excluded throughout. Box 21 is
excluded from the vertical evaporation sign as a catastrophic leak.

**Evaporation fell eleven-fold**, to 0.077 g/cage/day for the tilted geometry against
0.854 g/cage/day for the 2 mL design, and **volume at risk fell twenty-fold**, 0.4 mL
loaded per cage against 8 mL.

**The result that makes the first two safe: consumption was unchanged.** The tilted
geometry returned 15.7–19.2 µL/bee/day against the 16.7 µL/bee/day benchmark from the
2 mL design. An eleven-fold reduction in evaporation was achieved **without altering
what the animal drinks**. Had consumption fallen, the improvement would have been
indistinguishable from the bees simply being unable to feed.

**The vertical geometry leaks.** Leak annotations were recorded on nine of the
vertical cages and on none of the tilted cages. Apparent consumption in the vertical
arm runs ~45% above the tilted arm at 20G and 21G, which is the leak being weighed as
intake — the same failure mode as evaporation in B1, in a different disguise. The
vertical geometry was rejected.

**Port diameter.** Within the tilted geometry, consumption rose monotonically as bore
narrowed: 15.7 → 17.2 → 19.2 µL/bee/day across 20G → 21G → 23G. The direction is
counter-intuitive until read as retention rather than flow; a wider port loses
solution by wicking and dripping before it can be drunk. Bench annotations agree:
tube-emptying was recorded on one 20G cage and on six 21G/23G cages. **23G was
adopted.**

**A capacity rule that predicts its own failure mode.** At 19.2 µL/bee/day, fifteen
bees require **288 µL/day** against the **400 µL** held in two PCR tubes — 72% of
capacity consumed daily. Any uneven draw between the two tubes therefore empties one
before the next change, which is exactly what was annotated ("both basically empty",
23G). **Design rule for re-use: three tubes per cage at 23G, or sub-24 h
replacement.**

**Mortality.** 5 deaths in 270 bees over two days (1.9%) against 12 in 210 over three
days (5.7%) on the 2 mL design. The observation windows differ; per bee-day the
figures are 0.9% and 1.9%.

**What we learnt.** The redesign reduced waste but **did not recover the
denominator**: intake per bee in a cage is still inferred from a cage mean. More
fundamentally, cage feeding cannot tell us how much each individual bee consumed, and
without a known dose per bee we could not test dsRNA stability in the bee or measure
how much dsRNA reaches the haemolymph.

**What it changed.** PCR-tube feeders in 3D-printed inserts at 23G became the
standard vessel — see [hardware](/hardware). But because the project was going in the
direction of accurately characterising dsRNA persistence within adult honeybee
haemolymph, we decided we needed a way to control the volume of solution fed to each
bee, and moved to individual harnessed feeding at
[cycle B3](/engineering#cycle-b3).
