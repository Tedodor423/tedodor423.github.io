# Second DBTL — proboscis-extension assay testing

**Question.** Cage feeding can only ever give a cage average
([cycle B1](/engineering#cycle-b1)). Can we feed a known volume to each individual
bee instead, reliably enough to state a dose per animal?

**Design.** The proboscis-extension response assay, on the advice of our bee lab
PI: bees are strapped into tubes with head and thorax protruding, not harmed, and
fed from the tip of a 19-gauge syringe. Its usual purpose is mortality testing; we
wanted it as a dosing instrument. The first setup existed only to answer whether the
worker bees we caught would feed on our solutions at all.

**Build, in two iterations.**

1. **The pilot.** A single tray of 20 worker bees, each offered 5 µL of 1 M sucrose
   solution from the syringe.
2. **2.5 µL increments.** Feed **2 × 2.5 µL** rather than one 5 µL drop, to reach
   the same 5 µL total — at 200 ng/µL dsGFP that is a **1 µg dose per bee**. At most
   18 bees per session (five replicates for each of three lengths, plus three
   controls), with each length group started **20 minutes** apart.

**Test.** Iteration 1: whether caught workers would take the droplet at all.
Iteration 2: run as the dosing protocol for the full adult-haemolymph stability
assay — see [the bee lab](/bee-lab) and
[experiments and protocols](/bee-lab-experiments).

**Result.** The pilot failed as a dosing method. Many droplets were dropped or
absorbed onto the surface of the bees, and many of the workers escaped. From our own
observations and advice from apiarists at the bee lab, we determined that
**newly-emerged nurse bees**, while more difficult to obtain, would both relate more
closely to the pathway we are characterising and likely increase uptake. Foragers
took up to ~30 µL but only **22 of 50 survived the day**; newly emerged bees are
calm, harness easily and take a controlled **5–10 µL**. Throughput settled at 20
bees strapped and fed per person per hour. Iteration 2 was the most effective
protocol we found for consistently getting a known volume and concentration into an
adult bee.

**What we learnt.** A larger dose delivered to an animal that dies before its
timepoint is not a larger dose, so volume was **the wrong quantity to maximise**.
Splitting the drop in two is what made the volume accurate: a single 5 µL droplet is
large enough to roll off or soak into the bee. And the bee you choose is a design
variable, not a supply constraint — newly emerged bees are harder to obtain and
better on every axis that matters here.

**Limitation.** The staggering interval is recorded two ways in our own write-up:
20 minutes in the assay method and in the list of experimental difficulties, 15
minutes in this cycle's own table. The wiki uses 20 throughout.

**What it changed.** Newly-emerged bees and PER feeding became standard for every
adult dosing experiment on this project, which is what makes a µg-per-bee figure
mean anything, and the 20-minute stagger is what keeps the 1 h and 3 h timepoints
honest given about 15 minutes of setup per batch.
