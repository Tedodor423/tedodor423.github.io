# Should _E. coli_ be the production host?

**Question.** Low yield is yeast's weakness. Could _E. coli_, the field default, make
enough dsRNA to spike into a pollen patty instead?

**Design.** _E. coli_ HT115(DE3) is the standard dsRNA factory: the RNase III-null
strain gives full interference in >98% of _C. elegans_ progeny against <15% for
RNase III-competent BL21(DE3) `[LIT]`. The economics are good, at 53.3 µg/mL and
~US$4.29/mg `[LIT]`. `[CALC]` At that titre a 1 L shake flask yields ~53 mg dsRNA,
and Muita dosed at ~1 µg per bee, so one preparation covers a full dose-response
plus survival series. **Cost is not the constraint.**

Inducible T7 systems are often used to maximise microbial dsRNA expression, and we
designed one further step on top: **T7-driven expression of the insert combined with
rifampicin treatment.** Rifampicin inhibits the native _E. coli_ RNA polymerase and
leaves only T7 polymerase active, so native RNA synthesis stops, the endogenous
bacterial RNA already present is degraded away, and over time only T7-driven
transcripts accumulate. The same design carries a comparison we wanted anyway: two
inserts compatible with the same plasmid, one single-promoter loop-ended and one
dual-promoter, to see whether the mode of expression changes the level.

**This is a design. It was never run.** No rifampicin experiment appears anywhere in
our lab record, and we make no claim about what that combination would yield.

**Build.** The loop-ended insert was cloned and used to transform _E. coli_ **BL21**.
Otherwise _E. coli_ was a cloning host throughout and not a production host: two
transformations of Gibson product were performed (3 Sep, 7 Sep) under kanamycin
selection.

**Test and result.** **No dsRNA titre was measured in _E. coli_.** No colony counts or
colony-PCR outcomes were logged for either transformation, and the plasmid is unnamed
in the record. **We do not claim an _E. coli_ dsRNA production result.**

**What we learnt.** The decisive argument against _E. coli_ as our production host is
not yield and not cost, it is molecular: full-length hairpin RNA is **degraded in
HT115**, and RNase III deficiency is not sufficient to prevent it `[LIT]`. Knocking
out further RNases to fix that is a project of its own and beyond what we could do
inside iGEM. The best-established bacterial chassis is disqualified by the specific
molecule we chose in [cycle 1.2](/engineering#cycle-1-2). The yield advantage also
comes attached to a purification step, which costs money, loses product and brings
its own regulatory questions — none of which a whole-cell yeast formulation has.

**What it changed.** Confirmed the chassis decision of
[cycle 2.2](/engineering#cycle-2-2) from a second, independent direction, and
reframed _E. coli_ in our workflow as **a cloning and protein-expression host only**.
It returns in [cycle 5.2](/engineering#cycle-5-2) for MCP purification.
