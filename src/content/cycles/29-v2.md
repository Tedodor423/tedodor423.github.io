# Second DBTL — soaking mites in dsRNA and rearing them in live brood cells

**Question.** Mites survive best on a host in its natural setting
([cycle V1](/engineering#cycle-v1)). Does our dsRNA target increase mite mortality
relative to a saline control when the mite is soaked in it and then put into a live
larval cell?

**Design.** In our second iteration, we soaked mites in dsRNA and inserted them into
live larval cells. This let us test whether our vdCHIB target increased mite
mortality relative to a saline control. **With limited resources, we chose to hedge
our bets on this new method**: we did not run a comparison assay between it and other
rearing methods, but used it directly to compare our target dsRNA solution against a
saline control, maximising sample size for each arm instead.

**Build.** Mites were soaked in 0.9% NaCl containing a **low** or a **high**
concentration of our 700 bp vdCHIB dsRNA, or in saline alone. Surviving mites were
inserted individually into capped larval cells and incubated for 24 h. **Sample sizes
were not equalised between groups**, because mite survival through each soak varied.

**Test.** Live and dead mites scored after 24 h.

**Result: mortality rose with dose.**

| Group              | Alive | Dead | Total | Mortality |
| ------------------ | ----- | ---- | ----- | --------- |
| Control (saline)   | 23    | 17   | 40    | **42.5%** |
| Low concentration  | 31    | 39   | 70    | **55.7%** |
| High concentration | 18    | 47   | 65    | **72.3%** |

**Statistics.** We used the **Cochran–Armitage trend test** as the primary analysis,
since the results came as an ordered dose series with a binary outcome, using all
three groups in a single test rather than splitting the data into separate pairwise
comparisons. This returned a clear significant trend, **Z = 3.08, p = 0.002**
`[CALC]`. Its strength is statistical power from pooling the full dataset into one
hypothesis test; its limitation is that with only three dose points it cannot
distinguish a linear dose-response from a threshold effect.

We supplemented this with **Fisher's exact tests on each pair of groups**, since
these check whether any specific pair is individually distinguishable rather than
just confirming an overall trend. Running the test three times on the same data
stacks the chance of a false positive, so we used a stricter threshold (0.0167
instead of 0.05). Against that threshold, **only the high-against-control comparison
held up**.

**The inference** is that there is some statistically significant evidence of a
dose-response relationship, and high concentration is confidently distinguishable
from control. The apparent difference between high and low concentration is
consistent with the overall trend but **not independently confirmed** at a corrected
significance level.

**What we learnt.** We had our first evidence of a dose-response to vdCHIB dsRNA, but
**the soaking step was a major weakness**. Only about 20% of mites survived it, and
soaked mites sometimes reproduced in the cell, leaving unexposed offspring alongside
the treated mother — so some scored mites had never met the dose.

**What it changed.** To bypass soaking entirely, we decided to deliver dsRNA **through
the host**, by feeding the larvae themselves — [cycle V3](/engineering#cycle-v3). A
key next step for this cycle as it stands would be to repeat it in a more favourable
mite season (early August) and run the 72 h mite titre with significantly larger,
equalised sample sizes for controls and doses, with a dsRNA concentration titre to
show a linear effect.
