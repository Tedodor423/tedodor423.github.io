# Third DBTL — does the dose persist long enough for a mite to meet it?

**Question.** _Varroa_ enters the cell just before capping and feeds on the larva
throughout the capped period. Does dsRNA fed to a 5th instar larva persist long
enough for an infesting mite to be exposed to it?

**Design.** To test this, we dosed larvae with a **700 bp GFP dsRNA**. GFP has no
biological silencing target, so as in the adult haemolymph stability assay it
isolates delivery and persistence from knockdown. Each larva received **1 µg of
dsRNA** (5 µL at 200 ng/µL, diluted with 15 µL of sucrose solution to 20 µL) —
the volume settled in [cycle L2](/engineering#cycle-l2). Controls received 20 µL of
sucrose solution alone from the same batch. The construct was produced by IVT and
checked by Qubit and gel before dosing.

**Build.** **Seventy-five 5th instar larvae were dosed across five timepoints** —
24 h, 48 h, 72 h, 96 h and 7 days — each with 10 dosed larvae and 5 sucrose-only
controls. Dosed and control cells were interleaved and randomised across the frame,
with zones allocated to timepoints in advance and a full cell map recorded before
dosing to avoid positional confounds.

**What we learnt.** A single 1 µg dose persists intact in 5th instar larvae **for at
least 24 h**. That spans the window when _Varroa_ enters the cell and begins feeding,
which is the window the whole delivery argument depends on.

**What it changed.** This confirmed larval feeding as a viable delivery route, and it
is the result the brood-frame mite death assay
([cycle V3](/engineering#cycle-v3)) was built on: dose the host rather than soak the
mite, so that the mite acquires dsRNA by feeding, as it would in the field.
