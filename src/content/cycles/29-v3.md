# Iteration 3 — dosing the larva, then infesting the cell

**Question.** The soak kills most mites and leaves the rest too slow to use
([cycle V2](/engineering#cycle-v2)), and larvae kept outside the hive die
([cycle V1](/engineering#cycle-v1)). Can we drop the soak, keep the host alive in its
colony, and still get dsRNA into the mite?

**Design.** An entirely different approach: dose the larva, not the mite, so that the
mite meets the dsRNA by feeding on its host, as it would in a colony. The design was
proposed on 11 September from a suggestion by our bee lab mentor, adapted by the
team. It avoids the soak-and-wait bottleneck and the mites lost to drowning, and with
enough target dsRNA it could give a dose-response between the larval dose and mite
mortality.

**Build.** 5th instar larvae are spiked in the frame as in the larval pilots
([cycle L1](/engineering#cycle-l1)), and the frame goes back into the hive for a day
so that the workers cap the cells. The frame is then taken out and live mites, fresh
from a sugar shake, are introduced by carefully uncapping and resealing each cell.
This needs much more care than our earlier attempts, because nurse bees remove
visibly damaged cells and pupae if the frame is out too long.

**What we learnt.** In this pilot no soak was needed, the host larvae survived, and
background mite mortality was low: the three things that had failed in cycles V1 and
V2. It is also the most realistic of the methods we tried. It became the screen for
our SEED-designed constructs, [cycle V4](/engineering#cycle-v4).
