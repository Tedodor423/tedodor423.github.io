# Does a one-chain MS2 adaptor still grip the hairpin?

**Question.** Can a structure-prediction workflow reproduce the published MS2 coat
protein–RNA complex, and does the adaptor we would actually express, a
capsid-defective coat protein with both halves on a single chain, keep that
geometry?

**Design.** MCP recognises the MS2 operator as a dimer, which is why a hairpin in
the RNA plus an MCP fusion is the standard way to recruit a protein cargo to a
tagged RNA `[LIT]`. Two properties of wild-type MCP are wrong for an adaptor: it is
a viral coat protein and polymerises into capsids, and a dimer of two separate
chains means expressing two polypeptides and trusting them to pair. So we raised the
complexity in stages rather than modelling the finished construct in one step, and
scored each stage against the one before it. Stage A is wild type. Stage B adds the
V75E/A81G double mutation, which LeCuyer et al. report as showing no measurable
cooperative binding of the sort associated with capsid formation while keeping
operator recognition comparable to wild type `[LIT]`. Stage C joins the two
protomers with a GSTSGSG linker, after Shechner et al. 2015, into a 265 aa tandem
dimer we call tdMCP: protomer 1 at residues 1–129, the linker at 130–136, protomer 2
at 137–265 `[LIT]`.

**Build.** Every prediction used Boltz2 at frozen settings: Boltz 2.2.1, model
boltz2, 5 diffusion samples, 200 sampling steps, 3 recycling steps, seed 42, MSA
server enabled, full PAE and PDE, pLDDT, mmCIF output. The RNA is the 19 nt operator
`ACAUGAGGAUCACCCAUGU`, the C(−5) variant present in 1ZDH, and 1ZDH itself, solved by
X-ray crystallography at 2.7 Å, is the experimental reference throughout.

**Test.** We kept all five diffusion samples per construct rather than only the
most confident one, aligned each on the reconstructed 1ZDH complex, and measured
protein Cα RMSD, RNA RMSD after aligning the MCP dimer, recovery of the 39 reference
protein–RNA contacts (heavy-atom pairs within 4.5 Å in the reference), additional
non-reference contacts, and severe clashes (any inter-group heavy-atom pair under
2.0 Å).

**Result: both modifications leave the binding geometry intact.**

| Stage            | Reference-like models | Protein-aligned RNA RMSD      | Reference contacts recovered |
| ---------------- | --------------------- | ----------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| A, wild type     | 2/5                   | 0.656 and 0.700 Å             | 32/39 and 33/39              |
| B, V75E/A81G     | **5/5**               | 0.480–0.789 Å, median ~0.730  | 31–34/39, retention ~0.846   |
| C, tdMCP, 265 aa | **5/5**               | 0.674–1.511 Å, median ~0.852  | 30–32/39, retention ~0.795   |

The three Stage A models that were not reference-like placed the RNA about 21.5 Å
from its experimental position and recovered none of the 39 contacts. The five
predictions did not form a gradient from good to bad placement: they separated into
two states, and that split comes back at every later stage, which is why we report
a count out of five rather than a mean. Stage B produced 4–5 extra contacts and no
severe clashes. Stage C produced 3–6 extra contacts and no detectable linker–RNA
interference once the peptide bonds joining the linker to the protomers were
excluded, with one model of the five carrying both an MCP–MCP and an MCP–RNA clash
while still holding the reference-like RNA placement. We read that one as a warning
flag on that prediction, not as evidence the architecture cannot work.

**What we learnt.** Going from 2/5 to 5/5 is **not** evidence that the mutations
bind RNA better. Five diffusion samples are five outputs of a prediction model, not
measurements of affinity, so the defensible statement is the narrower one: V75E/A81G
and the covalent tandem dimer did not disrupt the experimentally supported binding
geometry. Everything here is computational, and a predicted construct that keeps the
reference geometry can still fail in a cell.

**What it changed.** The adaptor became a single 265 aa chain,
MCP(V75E/A81G)–GSTSGSG–MCP(V75E/A81G), rather than two wild-type protomers, which
also removes the unpaired-monomer problem that makes titre hard to define in
[5.2](/engineering#cycle-5-2). Stage C is the control every later construct is
scored against, so a change in RNA placement can be attributed to the passenger or
the linker rather than to the adaptor itself. The screen built on it is
[5.1c](/engineering#cycle-5-1c).
