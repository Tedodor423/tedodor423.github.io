# Iteration 3 — extracting haemolymph from adult bees

**Question.** Haemolymph is the fluid that circulates through an insect's body, its
equivalent of blood, and it is what _Varroa_ mites feed on: dsRNA that reaches the
haemolymph is dsRNA a mite can take up. Every adult measurement on this wiki is
therefore a haemolymph measurement. How much can one bee give, and how do we get it
out fast and clean?

**Design.** A time-course feeding experiment is only accurate if haemolymph comes out
of every bee rapidly and pure. Bleeding a bee through a puncture gives microlitres
contaminated with gut content and fat body, and the crop and gut can still hold
undigested dsRNA, which would read as a false positive. We wanted a method fast
enough to run on 40 to 60 bees in a session, that gives a known volume, and that gets
**as close to 20 µL of pure haemolymph per bee** as an adult worker can give.

**Build.** Chill the cage until the bees slow. Cut the abdomen off at the thorax
junction, **the petiole, between segments A1 and A2**, so crop and gut contents stay
behind, and cut the antennae at the base or mid-section, which lets the haemolymph
out of the head capsule. Place the head-and-thorax fragment head-first into a custom
extraction tube nested in a collection tube, and spin briefly in a pre-cooled
centrifuge. Spin the pooled sample again to pellet debris, take a known volume of
supernatant onto ice, and store at −80 °C so that the dsRNA it carries is not
degraded by RNases. First run on 3–4 July; the full protocol is on
[experiments and protocols](/bee-lab-experiments).

**Test.** Volume recovered per bee, across sessions of 40 or more bees.

**Result.** **8–15 µL of clean haemolymph per adult bee**, reliably, with typical
session yields at the lower end, 5–10 µL (26–27 August). Yield varied between bees;
we linked it tentatively to exactly where the antenna was cut, tip against base, and
did not resolve it.

**What we learnt.** The unresolved yield variation is why calibration material is
pooled rather than run per bee: pooling absorbs **a variation we could not remove**.
For the same reason a labelled aliquot is kept from every bee before pooling, because
pooling without concentrating buys a mean and no sensitivity.

**Limitation.** Volume, not purity, was measured. The protein load that defeats the
in-gel Mango assay ([cycle 4.1](/engineering#cycle-4-1)) is present **in every one of
these samples**.

**What it changed.** Fixed the sample volume that every rt-qPCR and Mango cycle is
designed around, and supplied the unspiked matrix for the Mango calibration curve:
60 newly emerged bees bled on 26–27 August for that purpose alone.
