# First DBTL — the most effective assay to feed adult bees a dsRNA-containing sucrose solution

**Question.** How much dsRNA does a bee actually receive? Every µg-per-bee figure
on this wiki depends on the answer, and a cage of bees sharing a feeder does not
give one.

**Design.** Measure what a cage of bees consumes from a shared feeder and divide
by the number of bees. It is cheap, and it gives a mean. One prior question had to
be settled first: whether the salt our dsRNA stock carries would stop bees drinking
the solution, or harm them.

**Build, in three iterations.**

1. **Cage feeding, standard tubes.** Four 2 ml Eppendorf feeding tubes weighed on a
   lab balance, filled with 1 M sucrose, fitted into a box of 15 bees, left in the
   incubator and re-weighed every 24 h. Fifteen boxes: five on plain sucrose, three
   at each of 50, 100 and 150 mM NaCl, and **one with no bees** as the evaporation
   control. Dead bees were counted at every solution change and subtracted from the
   per-bee arithmetic.
2. **PCR-tube adaptors.** Filling a cut-down Eppendorf to the brim wastes more
   dsRNA in dead volume than a session of IVT produces. We down-scaled to a
   standard PCR tube held in a custom 3D-printed insert.
3. **Five tube forms compared.** Horizontal, tilted and vertical PCR tubes, with the
   20-gauge feeding hole in the top or in the bottom, at 250 µl of 1 M sucrose each
   — first as an evaporation test with **no bees in any cage**, because on a 24 h
   weight difference evaporation and spillage _are_ the measurement, then in
   mini-cages of 15 bees across five adaptor combinations with two repeats of four
   tubes.

**Test.** Sucrose uptake by mass balance, and 24 h mortality.

**Result.** **Salt is not a constraint.** Sucrose consumption did not differ across
0, 50, 100 and 150 mM NaCl — one-way ANOVA on cage means, **F(3,10) = 0.96,
p = 0.45** — and 24 h mortality was not associated with it: 3 deaths in 210 bees,
**chi-square = 4.85 on 3 df, p = 0.18** `[CALC]`. **The vessel comparison produced
nothing usable**: two of four candidate designs failed on handling before the first
24 h reading, and the surviving tilted and vertical pair could not be separated from
their own leaking evaporation controls. Background mortality over a weekend was
0/15, 1/16 and 2/15 across three cage boxes (20 Jul).

**What we learnt.** Cage feeding gives a population mean and cannot support a
per-bee figure, however good the feeder. That, not the leaking, is why we stopped
refining the vessel: even a perfect one would still have given us a cage average.

**Limitation.** The tube forms were compared **on sucrose, not on dsRNA solution**,
and the comparison has no usable numbers at all — the per-tube masses live only in
the team spreadsheet. The weekend figures (0/15, 1/16, 2/15) are background
mortality; the salt comparison is the chi-square above, and three of its four
expected cell counts fall below 5.

**What it changed.** PCR-tube feeders in 3D-printed inserts became the standard
vessel, and the salt result cleared our dsRNA stock buffer for use in every later
feeding experiment. The vessel-optimisation assay was **abandoned rather than
iterated a third time**, and we moved to individual feeding — the
proboscis-extension assay at [cycle B2](/engineering#cycle-b2), which is the only
route that yields a dose per animal. Larval dosing moved to in-frame spiking for the
same reason: the larva stays in the colony and the dose is applied to its cell.
