# Iteration 1 — Mango on crude haemolymph extract

**Question.** Can we build a dsRNA that reports its own concentration and integrity
in crude haemolymph, with no purification, no labelled nucleotide and no blot?

**Design.** Every existing method for seeing dsRNA in bee material needs either a
blot or a chemically-labelled nucleotide. Our first candidate came from outside the
team: Prof. Jeffrey Barrick, coauthor of the paper that engineered _S. alvi_ to
produce dsRNA, suggested a **split-YFP dsRNA sensor** — dsRNA-binding domains from
different viruses fused to fluorescent proteins, faster and more readily engineerable
than an aptamer. We rejected it for crude haemolymph before building anything,
because it is not construct-specific: **the background from non-construct dsRNA
binding would swamp the signal**.

We wanted instead a tag that is genetically encoded in the plasmid, one copy, at a
defined position, at no marginal cost, so the same construct works for IVT now and
for in-yeast expression later. **Mango-II** is a 41 nt fluorogenic RNA aptamer that
binds the fluorophore TO1-Biotin with **Kd 1.1 ± 0.3 nM** and raises its fluorescence
**more than 1,500-fold** (Trachman 2018) `[LIT]`. It was the right variant for three
reasons: that enhancement, a dissociation constant low enough to be sensitive at the
concentrations we work at, and the fact that it folds as a hairpin — so it drops
straight into a terminal loop of our dumbbell instead of being bolted on.

A plate reader cannot tell us what length the construct was when it fluoresced, so we
developed a second read-out alongside it: resolve the constructs on a gel, then stain
the gel with TO1-Biotin.

**Build.** Mango sits in one terminal loop of the ledRNA construct (the L1 part),
with the 300/500/700 bp GFP series ± Mango as substrate. On the dual-promoter
constructs Mango was added by PCR; the loop-ended Mango dumbbell was ordered as a
synthesised fragment ([cycle 1.2](/engineering#cycle-1-2)). Two readouts were
developed in parallel: a 96-well plate assay and an in-gel post-stain.

**Test.** Calibration series in water and in spiked adult haemolymph, triplicates,
water blanks, on a plate reader and two gel imagers.

**Result: in crude haemolymph, nothing.** The calibration we set out to build spiked
adult haemolymph with known dsRNA standards against an unspiked haemolymph control.
After incubation with the K⁺ folding buffer and TO1-Biotin, the plate reader gave
**high background fluorescence and no significant difference between the spiked
samples and the controls.** The gel route then showed part of why: on a native gel
post-stained with TO1-Biotin, with no dye cast into the gel itself, **even the DNA
ladder stained.**

**Result: run 1 looked excellent, and that is the problem.** A linear fit over the
lowest four points gave **slope 4,952 RFU per ng/µL, R² = 0.9999, CVs 0.8–7.4%, LOD
0.23 ng/µL, LOQ 0.69 ng/µL** `[CALC]`. Run 2, at 125 nM dye with no Tween-20, was
**non-monotonic**, varied 2.6-fold in response within a single run, and degraded
LOD and LOQ roughly sixfold.

**What we learnt.** We initially reported run 1 as a
successful Mango calibration. **On re-analysis it was not measuring the aptamer.**
TO1's dissociation constant for duplex RNA is ~1–10 µM, and run 1 was performed at
1 µM TO1-Biotin, where intercalated dye along a 700 bp duplex dominates the signal
`[CALC]`. Intercalation is rigorously linear in dsRNA mass. **R² = 0.9999 is exactly
what a pure-intercalation signal produces, so a perfect calibration line was
evidence against the aptamer working, not for it.**

**Three independent confirmations of the same conclusion.** First, the control that
decides it: a tagged and an untagged construct, run on the same gel at matched mass
and post-stained together, **stained indistinguishably — and a commercial ladder,
stained alone, stained too.** A ladder carries no Mango aptamer at any position, so
its staining cannot be attributed to aptamer binding under any model of affinity or
stoichiometry.

Second, the arithmetic. Densitometry on the 592 bp construct gives an in-gel
**LOD of 0.42 ng in water, which is 1.10 fmol, against a published in-gel Mango-II
detection limit of 62.5 fmol** `[LIT]` — **57× below** the limit of the mechanism
supposedly producing the signal. Third, a Mango aptamer base-paired inside a duplex
does not fluoresce, with base-pairing reported to suppress signal 78–234-fold
`[LIT]`.

Three further failure mechanisms are quantified rather than glossed: flavin
autofluorescence sits almost exactly on the detection channel (ex ~440–450, em
~510–550 nm) `[LIT]`; a 700 bp duplex presents 125–250 intercalation sites against
one Mango site `[CALC]`; and adult haemolymph at 12.3–32.4 mg/mL protein `[LIT]`
means 98–536 µg of protein against 0.408 µg of dsRNA in an 8 µL lane, so we had run
an unintentional EMSA with the entire bee proteome as the shift partner. That last
one is **retention, not degradation**: the band stayed at the correct migration
position, and degradation would have given a downward smear.

**Correction.** An earlier version of this analysis claimed intercalation outshines
Mango by 110–140×. That was a saturation calculation applied to a sub-saturating
regime, and **we retract it.**

**Decision.** **Mango is demoted from quantifier to selector.** As a detector of
mass, an ordinary intercalating stain wins by orders of magnitude and always will:
one fluorophore per 410 kDa against one per 2–4 bp is not a gap you optimise away.
What Mango can still do is answer the questions that need sequence specificity,
tagged against untagged, and selective capture.

**Novelty claim, narrowed.** Mango has already been used
for quantification in a crude biological matrix `[LIT]`. Our claim is the
intersection of three things: amplification-free, direct
Mango quantification of dsRNA (not a fused tag) in an insect matrix.

**What it changed.** Mango reassigned from quantifier to selector, and the crude
haemolymph format abandoned: with this much background and this much intercalation,
the matrix has to be removed rather than corrected for, which is
[cycle 4.2](/engineering#cycle-4-2). Mass measurement moved to a generic stain
([cycle 4.3](/engineering#cycle-4-3)) and quantitative numbers to RT-qPCR
([cycle 3.3](/engineering#cycle-3-3)).
