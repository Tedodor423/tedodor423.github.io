# Iteration 3 — extracting whole larvae

**Question.** Haemolymph extraction does not scale
([cycle L2](/engineering#cycle-l2)). Is there a quick, reliable way to sample larvae
that works on capped cells and on tens of larvae per timepoint?

**Design.** Take the whole larva instead of its haemolymph. Dosing at the 5th instar
means every timepoint falls at or beyond capping, so each sample is harvested from a
capped cell, uncapped at collection, and processed as a whole animal. The larvae are
the same 5th instar and capped larvae as in [cycle L2](/engineering#cycle-l2).

**Build.** Uncap the cell without touching the larva, place a small piece of dry ice
in the cell against it until it is frozen solid, then lift it out: a frozen larva is
far less likely to burst. It goes straight into a labelled tube on dry ice, and into
a −80 °C freezer within 2 hours. The full protocol is on
[experiments and protocols](/bee-lab-experiments).

**Test.** Whether larvae can be taken from the hive reliably, capped and uncapped,
at the numbers a time-course needs.

**Result.** Flash-freezing in the cell let us process high numbers of larvae, capped
ones included. It is not loss-free: even frozen, **30–80% of larvae burst during
extraction**, depending on the handler's skill, so more larvae have to be fed than
are needed. The first two whole larvae, dosed with 1 µg at the 5th instar and
flash-frozen 24 h later (31 July), gave intact RT-PCR signal (4 August).

**What we learnt.** Whole larvae give up the clean, extraction-free sample that
[cycle L2](/engineering#cycle-l2) was after. A whole larva is a dirty matrix, full of
contaminants that one-step RT-qPCR does not tolerate, so every sample needs an RNA
extraction step before measurement. And because the gut comes with it, **dsRNA
detected in a whole larva may still be in the gut rather than the haemolymph**.

**What it changed.** Whole-larva sampling is what made the larval persistence
time-course possible: 700 bp GFP dsRNA, 1 µg in 20 µL, fed to 5th instar larvae in
the hive, which were then harvested whole at five timepoints from 24 h to 7 days (10
dosed larvae and 5 sucrose-only controls at each) and extracted with a Vezol tissue
protocol. **Intact dsRNA was recovered 24 h after dosing**, which spans the window
when _Varroa_ enters the cell and begins feeding, but the levels were low: **most of the 1 µg dose had gone within 24 h**, which we
attribute to poor dsRNA stability in the feeding medium and in the hive. The
time-course started on 18 August and its later timepoints are not yet written up,
so this supports persistence **to 24 h** and no further. The design is on
[the bee lab](/bee-lab#larval-assays) and the data on
[results](/results#dsrna-is-still-detectable-intact-in-larvae-at-24-h-but-most-of-a-1-g-dose-is-gone).
It is also what the brood-frame mite assays
([cycle V3](/engineering#cycle-v3) and [cycle V4](/engineering#cycle-v4)) are built
on: dose the host rather than soak the mite, so the mite acquires dsRNA by feeding,
as it would in the field.
