# Iteration 2 — extracting larval haemolymph

**Question.** To measure dsRNA in a larva we need a sample out of it, and the needle
of [cycle L1](/engineering#cycle-l1) burst them. Larvae are soft-bodied, essentially
a bag of liquid and fat. Can we take clean haemolymph from them, without bursting
them, at the numbers a time-course needs?

**Design.** We wanted a pure haemolymph sample, because a clean sample could go
straight into one-step RT-PCR, or into [Mango](/engineering#cycle-4-1), with no
purification step. We discussed the approach with Prof. Geraldine Wright before
starting.

**Build.** Method established on 8 July. A 5th instar larva is lifted from the frame
with tweezers; blunt ones are safer, because pointed ones puncture it and the
haemolymph is lost instantly. A vertical ventral incision just below the head
segment, gentle pressure to express the haemolymph (pinched between two fingers, or
pressed flat on the dissection board with bent tweezers), and a P20 pipette to draw
it into an Eppendorf on ice. An entomological pin gave the purest, least
lipid-diluted sample. We tried it on uncapped 5th instar larvae and on capped ones at
the first day of pupation. Full steps are on
[experiments and protocols](/bee-lab-experiments).

**Test.** Whether haemolymph could be extracted reliably, and whether it came out
pure.

**Result.** On 29 July, 10 larvae dosed the day before gave about 10 µL each, from
5 to 11 µL, and one sample was contaminated by submersion in brood food. On 31 July,
two 5 µL draws per larva gave inconsistent yields, about 6 µL from one larva and
about none from another. **5th instar larvae could be extracted without breaking; 6th
instar larvae were too soft**, and capped larvae were harder still, bursting before
the haemolymph could be taken.
Later pupal stages are firmer but have lost the osmotic pressure that makes
haemolymph ooze out.

**What we learnt.** The method works on a single, careful larva. But larvae are hard
to extract reliably because they are soft, capped larvae are harder still, and the
method is labour-intensive: a time-course needs 10 larvae per dose per timepoint,
and processing tens of larvae this way was not practical.

**What it changed.** We stopped sampling larval haemolymph and moved to whole larvae,
[cycle L3](/engineering#cycle-l3).
