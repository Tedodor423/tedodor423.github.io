# Second DBTL — the structural arrangement of the larval spiking assay

**Design.**

- Larvae soft and prone to bursting → difficult to handle.
- Tried several haemolymph extraction methods.
- Removing larvae from frame difficult.
- RT-PCR analysis of obtained haemolymph samples → no dsRNA detected.
- Decision to process whole larvae instead.
- Whole larva removed from frame → flash frozen → stored for RNA extraction and
  RT-qPCR.
- Additional challenge of capping.
- Need to identify which larvae had been fed.
- Need to remove fed larvae after cells capped.

**Build.**

- Develop whole-larva sampling workflow.
- Dosed and control cells interleaved across the frame and **randomised by
  location**, with frame zones allocated to timepoints in advance and a full cell
  map recorded before dosing, so that treatment group and physical position on the
  frame could not be confounded.
- Remove whole larvae from frame.
- Flash freeze and store for downstream RNA extraction / RT-qPCR.

**Test.**

- Feed whole larvae required dose.
- Return one day later → larvae capped.
- Remove larvae from capped cells.
- Store for downstream processing.

**What we learnt.**

- Haemolymph extraction difficult and unreliable.
- Large number of samples required: 10 per dose per timepoint.
- Whole-larva processing faster and more reliable.
- **The capping schedule forces it as well as the extraction difficulty.** Dosing at
  5th instar means every timepoint falls at or beyond capping, so each sample is
  harvested as a capped cell, uncapped at collection, and processed as a whole
  animal rather than by in-cell haemolymph extraction alone.
- Decision to use whole larvae for downstream RNA extraction and RT-qPCR.
