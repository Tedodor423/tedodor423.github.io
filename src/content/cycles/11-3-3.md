# Iteration 2 — qPCR from adult and larval feeding experiments

**Question.** Can RT-qPCR give a defensible concentration of dsRNA in bee
haemolymph, and what does it say about how long a dose lasts?

**Design.** Two feeding experiments, one per life stage. **Adults** were fed 1 µg
doses of the 300, 500 and 700 bp GFP constructs, to test uptake across the gut and
stability in haemolymph against construct length. **Larvae** were fed 1 µg of the
700 bp construct, to test stability over the period of capping — the stage at which
nurse bees seal a larva into its cell for pupation, and from which the adult emerges
about ten days later. That window is also where _Varroa_ does its damage, so it is
the one that matters for a product.

RT-qPCR is the orthogonal method every other assay here is benchmarked against, so it
has to meet MIQE minimums: slope, y-intercept, efficiency, R², melt-curve
specificity, no-template Cq and no-RT Cq `[LIT]`. Two constraints are specific to
dsRNA work. **Primers must sit outside the dsRNA fragment**, because residual input
dsRNA carries into the RNA prep and, in a dose-response, carryover scales with dose,
producing an artefact that looks like the exact opposite of knockdown `[LIT]`. And
**denaturation is mandatory, but our construct breaks the usual fix**: in a
conventional duplex, heat-then-dilute works because two separate strands must find
each other again, whereas in a dumbbell the arms are covalently tethered, so
re-annealing is intramolecular and first-order and dilution cannot help
`[FLAG, inference from topology, not measured]`.

The two sample routes of [cycle 3.1](/engineering#cycle-3-1) follow from the tissue:
adult haemolymph goes into a one-step reaction after PTU and dilution, larval
homogenate through column extraction first.

**Build.** Primers were designed against a **conserved 100 bp region of GFP** present
in all three constructs, tiling 100 bp windows across them, and each route got its own
calibration curve. For adults the standards were spiked into **pooled untreated
haemolymph** carrying PTU at 0.1 mM, so the curve accounts for the matrix the samples
arrive in; for larval extracts the standards were diluted **in water**, which is what
an extracted sample resembles. Standards were Qubit-tested dsRNA
([cycle 2.6](/engineering#cycle-2-6)), not NanoDrop values.

For the _Varroa_ target, published primers for the target gene and its reference
genes, with SDHA as primary reference and NADH as a second. Actin was avoided, and 18S
used only as a secondary, because 18S sits at Ct 8–12 against a target Ct of 25–30.

**Test.** Serial dilution standard curves (200 ng to 0.02 ng in 1:10 steps, plus a
low-range series), triplicates throughout, melt curve on every run.

**Result: partial.** A standard curve was generated on 30 Jul, and needed a diagnostic
of its own: the 20–200 ng/µL points appeared to fail, but their Ct was so low that the
baseline had been set below the trend, and **resetting the baseline to 1 Ct**
recovered the curve. A temperature gradient across all primer pairs found a different
optimum for each and non-specific product for most; only one pair had a clean usable
Tm, so only that pair went forward. **No valid efficiency, slope, R², LOD or dynamic
range has yet been recorded to MIQE standard.** The only written internal acceptance
rule was R ≥ 0.9, which is below MIQE practice.

**In larvae, the majority of the 1 µg dose disappears within 24 hours** of feeding.
Poor dsRNA stability in the feeding medium and in the hive accounts for the low
recovery, and dose accuracy is part of the same problem: Qubit and NanoDrop disagreed
about what was in the feeding solution to begin with.

**What we learnt.** RT-qPCR is the benchmark and it is not the method to build a
product measurement on. Adult haemolymph melanises at variable rates, which shifts
background fluorescence between samples; the dsRNA titre in adult haemolymph is low
enough that an extraction step would lose most of it, and the one-step route that
avoids extraction does not transfer to most other biological material. RT also brings
amplification bias, and the equipment and reagents are expensive. That is the argument
for **a specific, purification-light, RT-free measurement**, which is
[cycle 4.1](/engineering#cycle-4-1).

Also worth a line: GFP dsRNA is our extraction spike, but it is a
**post-mortem** spike only. Fed GFP dsRNA alters around 1,400 bee genes
`[FLAG, source DOI unverified]`, so it is not an inert control in a live bee. It
remains the correct control in mite work.

**What it changed.** Forced the contamination diagnostic of
[cycle 3.3b](/engineering#cycle-3-3b), made spike-recovery a standing requirement,
and put the stability problem back on the molecule: most of a larval dose does not
last a day, which is what [cycle 1.2](/engineering#cycle-1-2) was designed against.
