# Iteration 2 — streptavidin fixation and wash

**Question.** Both failure modes in 3.4 — matrix autofluorescence and non-specific
TO1 intercalation — are properties of reading a crude sample directly. Can the
tagged construct be pulled out of the matrix first?

**Design.** We approached Prof. Peter Unrau, who discovered and characterised the
Mango aptamer. His suggestion converts a homogeneous assay into a capture assay:
TO1-Biotin can be fixed to streptavidin, so the Mango-tagged construct can be
pulled down and the haemolymph washed away. That **removes both failure modes at
once**, because weak intercalation washes off where a bound aptamer does not.
Prof. Unrau also suggested Mango III, which gives a stronger signal. **We chose to
stay with Mango II**, on the grounds that the eventual use is _in vivo_ and
Mango II is the more environmentally robust variant. That is a deliberate trade of
signal for durability and we record it as one.

**Build.** An assay to fix the Mango-tagged construct to streptavidin-bound
TO1-Biotin, wash away the unbound haemolymph matrix, and read the retained
fluorescence. The wash must use K⁺ and never Na⁺: Mango is a G-quadruplex, and the
cation is structural.

**Test and result.** Not yet read out.

**What we learnt.** The generalisable part is not the aptamer, **it is the format**.
Fixing the reporter to a solid phase makes the matrix irrelevant, which is what
would let the same measurement work in samples other than bee haemolymph. It also
gives Mango a second job it is actually suited to: selective capture and
purification of a tagged construct, and live-cell imaging, rather than mass
quantification.

**What it changed.** Mango's role on this project was redefined from quantifier to
capture handle, and the loop it sits in is retained in the L1 part for that reason
rather than as a reporter.
