# Which sequence silences the mite?

**Question.** Which _Varroa_ genes should we target, and which regions within those
genes should the dsRNA be made from? Previous studies had already demonstrated
_Varroa_-specific RNAi against several individual genes, but there was **no
standardised framework** for comparing candidate genes or for selecting the most
suitable region within a chosen gene.

**Design.** A three-stage strategy, built to be our own framework rather than
borrowed conventions.

1. **Target gene selection.** Rankings and pools of candidate genes on three
   independent axes: functional connectivity from a STRING analysis of the _Varroa_
   proteome, abundance from transcriptomics, and direct RNAi effects from the
   literature.
2. **Within-gene region selection.** A published _Varroa_ sRNA dataset first
   establishes the basic features of the mite's own sRNA population; the clearest
   pattern is that **23 and 24 nt sRNAs are particularly abundant**, so those two
   lengths become the basic units. Every possible 23 and 24 nt window in a candidate
   transcript is enumerated, and regions are selected by aggregating **three layers
   of evidence** across those units: whether a unit's sequence composition resembles
   sRNAs that are abundant in the _Varroa_ dataset; whether the guide sRNA has the
   biophysics of favourable strand selection and low self-folding; and whether the
   corresponding site in the target mRNA is accessible.
3. **Off-target analysis.** A region can score well inside _Varroa_ and still be
   identical or highly similar to transcripts in non-target species, particularly
   _Apis mellifera_. Selected regions are evaluated against the _Apis mellifera_
   transcriptome on two levels: strict mapping of ≥ 16 nt sequences, for the risk
   that local stretches produce off-target sRNAs, and whole-region similarity under
   relaxed mapping allowing substitutions and indels, for broader homology.

**Build.** For gene selection, protein interaction data from STRING measured
functional network connectivity, transcriptomic data ranked genes by abundance, and
published _Varroa_ RNAi studies were read manually to identify genes with previously
confirmed experimental knockdown.

For within-gene design, we took the raw FASTQ files from the _Varroa_ sRNA dataset,
ran QC and adapter cleanup, and mapped the sRNAs to viral genomes to identify and
analyse the features of virus-derived sRNAs. A sequence-analysis workflow then takes
an exact target transcript as input and enumerates every 23 and 24 nt window. The
three layers are computed as: a **regression model fitted to the viral sRNAs** of
that dataset, estimating abundance from sequence features; **guide-strand
thermodynamic asymmetry and self-folding with ViennaRNA**; and **RNAplfold** for the
accessibility of both the complete target site and the guide seed region within the
target mRNA.

Off-target analysis maps candidate regions to the _Apis mellifera_ transcriptome:
**Bowtie1** for strict mapping of sequences ≥ 16 nt, and **Edlib** for whole-region
mapping at set fractions of mismatches, insertions and deletions. The wider
non-target screen follows advice we were given directly, on choosing a justified set
of representative species rather than scanning everything; see
[attributions](/attributions).

The analysis scripts were then integrated into an **interactive user interface**, so
the complete workflow runs as one pipeline from gene selection through within-gene
target design to off-target analysis.

**Test.** A mite screen on the **unlooped** construct.

**Result.** Not returned. The screen sits at cycle V1, where the husbandry cycle
closed negative, so no efficacy data exists to test the pipeline's gene choice
against.

**What we learnt.** From conversations and literature review, that **changing
secondary structure affects potency**, and that the next thing to change was
therefore the architecture rather than the sequence: loop ends, designed for
stability.
