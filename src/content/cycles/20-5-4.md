# Does a protein ride the dsRNA across the bee gut?

**Question.** [5.1](/engineering#cycle-5-1) and
[5.3](/engineering#cycle-5-3) both assume a protein can be attached to the dsRNA and
still be there, and still be folded, on the far side of the bee gut. Is that
assumption testable now, without waiting for a Vg binder that does not exist?

**Design.** Replace the unknown cargo with a known one. GFP is a reporter whose
folding _is_ its signal, so a GFP that still fluoresces in haemolymph after being
fed as an RNA-protein complex **answers two questions at once**: the complex
survived the gut, and the protein survived with it. This is the cheap version of
[5.3](/engineering#cycle-5-3) with the risky half removed. The construct uses both
loops of the dumbbell: the MS2 hairpin array in one, the Mango aptamer in the other,
so the same molecule carries the adaptor site and the reporter the measurement work
on [measurement](/measurement) depends on.

**Build.** A cassette expressing MS2 coat protein linked to GFP with a His-tag,
expressed in _E. coli_ and purified, assembled onto loop-ended dsRNA carrying an MS2
hairpin array in one loop. Multiple MS2 hairpins were placed in an array in that
loop to ensure efficient MS2 binding, with the array geometry
[5.1](/engineering#cycle-5-1) arrived at. The linker between coat protein and
reporter is the one open variable, and the screen in
[5.1c](/engineering#cycle-5-1c) narrows it: of the eight conditions modelled with
sfGFP as the passenger, GGGGS4 and PAPAP each held the reference-like RNA geometry in
4 of 5 predictions, against 1 of 5 for GGGGS3.

**Test.** The readout as designed is short: feed the protein-dsRNA complex to adult
bees, extract haemolymph at 1 h, read GFP fluorescence on a plate reader, against
free GFP-MS2 with no dsRNA, undosed bees and buffer.

**Result.** None. Nothing in our record says this assay was run, and no
plate-reader values, control arms or n per arm exist anywhere we can cite.
**This wiki makes no claim that a functionalised dsRNA crossed the bee gut.**

**What we learnt.** The design lesson stands without the numbers: a reporter whose
signal depends on its own folding is the right cargo for a first-pass
functionalisation test, because it makes "did it survive" and "is it still working"
**the same measurement**.

**What it changed.** Nothing yet. If the assay runs and supports it, this is the
experiment that moves [5.3](/engineering#cycle-5-3) from Proposed to testable; if it
does not, the feeder demonstration in [5.3](/engineering#cycle-5-3) becomes the
primary route.
