# Can we get enough haemolymph out of an adult bee?

**Question.** Every measurement downstream needs adult haemolymph, in a volume the
assay can use and clean enough to read. How much can one bee give?

**Design.** Bleeding a bee through a puncture gives microlitres contaminated with
gut content and fat body. We wanted a method that is fast enough to run on 40 to 60
bees in a session and gives a known volume.

**Build.** Chill the cage until the bees slow. Cut the abdomen off at the
thorax junction and cut the antennae at the base — the antennal sockets are the
drain. Place the head-and-thorax fragment head-first into a custom extraction tube
nested in a collection tube, and spin briefly in a pre-cooled centrifuge. Spin the
pooled sample again to pellet debris, take a known volume of supernatant onto ice.

**Test.** Volume recovered per bee, across sessions of 40 or more bees.

**Result.** **8–15 µL of clean haemolymph per adult bee**, reliably, with typical
session yields at the lower end of 5–10 µL. Yield varied between bees; we linked it
tentatively to exactly where the antenna was cut, tip against base, and did not
resolve it.

**What we learnt.** The unresolved yield variation is the reason calibration
material is pooled rather than run per bee: pooling absorbs **a variation we could
not remove**. For the same reason a labelled aliquot is kept from every individual
bee before pooling, because pooling without concentrating buys a mean and no
sensitivity.

**Limitation.** Volume, not purity, was measured. The protein load that defeats the
in-gel assay in 3.4 is present **in every one of these samples**.

**What it changed.** Fixed the sample volume every measurement cycle in Workstream 3
is designed around, and supplied the unspiked matrix for the Mango calibration
curve: 60 newly emerged bees bled on 26–27 Aug for that purpose alone.
