# Iteration 2 — loop-ended dsRNA

**Question.** Most of a 1 µg larval dose is gone within 24 h, and one target did not
move mite mortality. Can one redesign answer both — a molecule that survives the hive
and silences several genes at once?

**Design.** Three changes, each with its own reason.

**Loop ends, for stability.** Muita et al. 2026 measured the problem in our exact
system: conventional dsRNA fell to ~25% of starting intensity within 3 h in 50%
sucrose and was undetectable by 6 h, while loop-ended dsRNA (ledRNA) **retained
~40–70% at day 2** and produced significant mite mortality where conventional dsRNA
produced none `[LIT]`. Terminal loops remove the free ends that exonucleases require.
Our own larval result in [cycle 1.1](/engineering#cycle-1-1) says the same thing from
the other direction. Stability matters to us specifically because **our administration
route is two handovers long**: the dsRNA has to be transferred into bees first and
into _Varroa_ second. In discussion with dsRNA methods experts, including **Sean
Leonard and Jeffrey E. Barrick**, loop ends were supported as the right approach for a
delivery system like ours. siRNA was excluded outright, because dietary RNAi in
insects needs dsRNA above ~60 bp `[LIT]`.

**Asymmetric bulges rather than wobble pairs.** Muita et al. also tested loop-ended
dsRNA carrying either **G–U wobble base pairs** or **asymmetric bulges (ASY)** in
_Varroa_. Both modified designs showed increased dsRNA persistence, which in turn
raised RNAi and mite mortality against conventional dsRNA. On our own later design
considerations we chose **ASY**.

**Multiplexing, for efficiency.** The first design silences one gene. Published work
shows that **multigene concatenation** constructs can carry several target sequences
in one dsRNA molecule, and sequence and secondary structure inform Dicer specificity,
so one long construct can yield the several siRNA populations we want. That turns the
question into which genes to combine, which sends us back to the ranked pools of
[cycle D1](/engineering#cycle-d1).

**Build.** The Muita geometry: a duplex stem flanked by two ~150 nt single-stranded
loops, transcribed as one RNA from a single T7 promoter.

**The two candidate pools, and the four genes each.** From D1 we took two pools. The
literature-derived **L pool** started from **65 gene × study observations**; we
retained direct-positive RNAi results and then kept only genes that could be
confidently mapped to exact _Varroa_ gene identities. The exploratory **E pool**
started from **5,408 genes**, filtered to those in the **top 5% for network
connectivity**, which left **299 genes**; those were grouped by biological function
and reviewed manually to pick representatives from different essential cellular
systems. **Four target genes from each pool**, one favourable **175 bp** region per
gene chosen with the SEED pipeline, and the four regions concatenated into a
**700 bp multigene construct** — one construct per pool.

**The ASY variant.** Following Muita et al.'s original construct: the antisense strand
carrying the target-complementary sequence is left unchanged, while **one nucleotide
is deleted from the opposing sense strand every 23 nt**. Each deletion leaves one
antisense nucleotide without a partner, giving consistent single-nucleotide asymmetric
bulges along the duplex stem. This is the construct written elsewhere on this wiki as
led[Δ23]: 670 nt of sense against 700 nt of antisense `[CALC]`.

**A modular collection, not one construct.** Inverted repeats are the specific thing
that breaks commercial gene synthesis and recombines out in standard cloning strains.
Rather than re-solve that per construct, we designed the loop as a socket so any
future cargo drops into a fixed backbone: **L0** a plain loop-ended dumbbell with no
cargo (the control, and the comparator that makes L1 an Improved Part), **L1** a Mango
reporter loop, **L2** an MS2 adaptor loop. Loop spacers are constrained to exclude
BsaI, BsmBI and BbsI sites, homopolymer runs longer than 3, and yeast poly(A)
elements. Detail on [parts](/results#parts).

**Test.** Folding assessed _in silico_ before synthesis (ViennaRNA 2.7.2, Turner
2004, 37 °C, partition function). Assembly checked by gel size of the step-1 product,
then PCR across the assembly with universal T7-U1 / U2-rev primers, then gel
extraction, re-amplification and sequencing. At the bench the two questions were
whether the mite result could be repeated with a multiplexed construct, and whether
loop ends improve stability _in vivo_.

**Result.** The 709 bp duplex arm is predicted paired at **p = 0.998–0.999** under
every perturbation we modelled `[CALC]`.

**Assembly failed, twice over.** Three gBlock fragments were assembled by two-step
Gibson — two fragments first, verified, then the third, because a three-part one-pot
that fails tells you nothing about which junction broke. Step 1 gave the expected
~500 bp product (5 Sep). Step 2 PCR gave a smear (7 Sep), diagnosed as incomplete
Gibson leaving ~180 bp U1/U2 fragments that amplify faster than the full product.
Three rounds of troubleshooting followed: residual ethanol removed from the gel
extract by dry-spinning with lids open; Q5 with GC enhancer at 1× and template at
5 ng/µL; a 57–63 °C annealing gradient. By 10 Sep, W5 gave a clean band at 60 °C and
W3 gave no band at any temperature, which told us **the wrong band had been excised**
from the earlier gel. **Sequencing then showed why the assembly would not close**:
short regions of complementarity between fragments that none of the software we
designed them with had predicted. Cross-over PCR failed for the same reason.
Ultimately we ordered whole fragments from a third-party synthesis company, and at
that cost **we could afford a single construct**.

**The mite re-test did not happen.** By early September mites had to be removed from
the hives to avoid colony collapse, so there was no window in which to repeat and
troubleshoot the mite experiments with a multiplexed construct.

**What we learnt.** Two lessons, one procedural and one strategic. The procedural one
is about **gel extraction, not Gibson**: an impure extract poisons downstream PCR, and
a temperature gradient distinguishes "the product is absent" from "the product is
outcompeted", which a single failed PCR cannot. The strategic one is that **a complex
dsRNA architecture cannot reliably be assembled from ordered parts** — inverted
repeats defeat both the synthesis vendors and the assembly chemistry, and the
fragments' own unpredicted complementarity defeated the design software. The time and
money spent on successive assembly and synthesis routes made coordinating wet lab with
bee lab the hardest scheduling problem of the project.

**Limitation.** **No construct assembled here was verified as correct**, and the one
loop-ended construct we work with was bought as a complete fragment. Δ23 is also
untested: Muita's published, _Varroa_-validated construct is led[Δ22], one deletion
every 22 nt, and ours is Δ23 on the reasoning that led[Δ22]-_Pero_ siRNAs peaked in
the 23–24 nt size class, so a 23 nt register may sit better on the Dicer product
distribution `[LIT]`. **We present Δ23 as a rationale, not a result** `[FLAG]`.

**What it changed.** Sanger sequencing across both junctions was added to the protocol
before any Registry submission, and a full re-run was adopted (Gibson → PCR → gel
extract → PCR → gel extract → IVT-ready, loading the entire sample at each
extraction). It also bounds our novelty claim: ledRNA against _Varroa_ is published.
Putting a functional cargo inside one of the loops is not.
