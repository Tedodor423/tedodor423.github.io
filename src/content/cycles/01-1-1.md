# Iteration 1 — model and initial design of the dsRNA

**Question.** Conventional dsRNA is destroyed in a hive feeder within hours. What
molecular architecture survives long enough to reach the mite?

**Design.** The failure that defines this project is persistence, not potency.
Muita et al. 2026 measured it in our exact system: conventional dsRNA fell to ~25%
of starting intensity within 3 h in 50% sucrose and was undetectable by 6 h, while
loop-ended dsRNA (ledRNA) **retained ~40–70% at day 2** and produced significant
mite mortality where conventional dsRNA produced none `[LIT]`. Terminal loops remove the
free ends that exonucleases require. siRNA was excluded outright, because dietary
RNAi in insects needs dsRNA above ~60 bp `[LIT]`.

Stability matters to us specifically because **our administration route is two
handovers long**: the dsRNA has to be transferred into bees first and into _Varroa_
second. In discussion with dsRNA methods experts, including **Sean Leonard and
Jeffrey E. Barrick**, loop ends were supported as the right approach for a delivery
system like ours.

**Efficiency, separately from stability.** The first design silences one gene. To
raise mite-killing efficiency we wanted to target several at once, and published
work shows that **multigene concatenation** constructs can carry multiple target
sequences in one dsRNA molecule. That turns the question into which genes to
combine, which sends us back to the ranked pools from D1.

**The bulge, and why ASY rather than wobble.** Muita et al. also tested loop-ended
dsRNA carrying either **G–U wobble base pairs** or **asymmetric bulges (ASY)** in
_Varroa_. Both modified designs showed increased dsRNA persistence, which in turn
raised RNAi and mite mortality against conventional dsRNA. On our own later design
considerations we chose **ASY**.

**Build.** The Muita geometry: a ~500 bp duplex stem flanked by two ~150 nt
single-stranded loops, transcribed as one RNA from a single T7 promoter.

**The two candidate pools, and the four genes each.** From D1 we took two pools.
The literature-derived **L pool** started from **65 gene × study observations**; we
retained direct-positive RNAi results and then kept only genes that could be
confidently mapped to exact _Varroa_ gene identities. The exploratory **E pool**
started from **5,408 genes**, filtered to those in the **top 5% for network
connectivity**, which left **299 genes**; those were grouped by biological function
and reviewed manually to pick representatives from different essential cellular
systems. **Four target genes from each pool**, one favourable **175 bp** region per
gene chosen with the D1 pipeline, and the four regions concatenated into a
**700 bp multigene construct** — one construct per pool.

**The ASY variant.** Following Muita et al.'s original construct: the antisense
strand carrying the target-complementary sequence is left unchanged, while **one
nucleotide is deleted from the opposing sense strand every 23 nt**. Each deletion
leaves one antisense nucleotide without a partner, giving consistent
single-nucleotide asymmetric bulges along the duplex stem. This is the construct
written elsewhere on this wiki as led[Δ23]: 670 nt of sense against 700 nt of
antisense `[CALC]`.

**Test.** Folding assessed _in silico_ before synthesis (ViennaRNA 2.7.2, Turner
2004, 37 °C, partition function), then by IVT yield and annealing behaviour at the
bench in 1.3.

**Result.** The 709 bp duplex arm is predicted paired at **p = 0.998–0.999** under
every perturbation we modelled `[CALC]`. The multigene loop-ended constructs were
**produced but not tested**: the bench comparison of ledRNA against conventional
duplex belongs to the bee lab, not here.

**What we learnt.** Architecture, not sequence, is the dominant lever on dsRNA
persistence in a hive, and it is a variable we control at the DNA level for no
marginal cost. It also bounds our novelty claim: ledRNA against _Varroa_ is
published. Putting a functional cargo inside one of the loops is not.

**What it changed.** An inverted-repeat, loop-ended molecule cannot be bought. That
forced 1.2.

> **Δ22 against Δ23, stated plainly.** Muita's published, _Varroa_-validated
> construct is led[Δ22], one deletion every 22 nt. Ours is Δ23, on the reasoning
> that led[Δ22]-_Pero_ siRNAs peaked in the 23–24 nt size class, so a 23 nt register
> may sit better on the Dicer product distribution `[LIT]`. **Δ23 is untested. We
> present it as a rationale, not a result** `[FLAG]`.
