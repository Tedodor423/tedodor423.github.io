# Can we deliver a known dose to a bee?

**Question.** How much dsRNA does a bee actually receive? Every µg-per-bee figure
on this wiki depends on the answer, and a cage of bees sharing a feeder does not
give one.

**Design.** Two routes, tried in that order. Measure what a cage consumes and
divide, or feed each bee individually and know. The first is cheap and gives a
mean; the second is slow and gives a dose per animal.

**Build, in four iterations.**

1. **Cage feeding, standard tubes.** Four feeding tubes weighed on a lab balance,
   filled with 1 M sucrose, fitted into a chamber of 30 bees, left in the incubator
   for 24 h and re-weighed. Dead bees were counted at every solution change and
   subtracted from the per-bee arithmetic.
2. **PCR-tube adaptors.** Filling a cut-down Eppendorf to the brim wastes more
   dsRNA in dead volume than a session of IVT produces. We down-scaled to a
   standard PCR tube held in a custom 3D-printed insert.
3. **Four tube geometries compared.** Vertical, flat, angled-down PCR tubes and the
   original perforated Eppendorf, all filled, weighed, fed for 24 h and re-weighed,
   with an evaporation control tube in every run — because on a 24 h weight
   difference, evaporation and spillage are the measurement.
4. **Proboscis extension, twice.** The first attempt fed 20 caught workers 5 µL
   each from a syringe: droplets were dropped or absorbed onto the bee's surface,
   and bees escaped the restraints. The second fed **2 × 2.5 µL** per bee to reach
   5 µL, at most 18 bees per session (five replicates for each of three lengths
   plus three controls), staggered 15 minutes per length group so extraction
   timepoints stay separated.

**Test.** Sucrose uptake by mass balance for the cage routes; uptake volume and
same-day survival for the two PER routes.

**Result.** Foragers took up to ~30 µL but only **22 of 50 survived the day**.
Newly emerged bees are calm, harness easily and take a controlled **5–10 µL**, and
at 1 µg in 5 µL that is a defined dose per animal. Throughput settled at 20 bees
strapped and fed per person per hour. Background mortality over a weekend was
0/15, 1/16 and 2/15 across three cage boxes (20 Jul).

**What we learnt.** A larger dose delivered to an animal that dies before its
timepoint is not a larger dose, so volume was **the wrong quantity to maximise**.
Cage feeding gives a population mean and cannot support a per-bee figure; PER
feeding is slower and is the only route that can. And the bee you choose is a
design variable, not a supply constraint: newly emerged bees are harder to obtain
and better on every axis that matters here.

**Limitation.** The four tube geometries were compared **on sucrose, not on dsRNA
solution**, and the journal does not record which cage box carried which NaCl
concentration, so the weekend figure is background mortality rather than a salt
tolerance comparison.

**What it changed.** Newly-emerged bees and PCR-tube feeders became standard for
every dosing experiment on this project, which is what makes a µg-per-bee figure
mean anything. Larval dosing moved to in-frame spiking for the same reason: the
larva stays in the colony and the dose is applied to its cell.
