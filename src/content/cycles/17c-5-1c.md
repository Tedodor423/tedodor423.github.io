# Which cargo, and which linker holds it on?

**Question.** The adaptor works in prediction on its own. Does a linker that holds
one cargo protein cleanly onto it hold the next one just as well?

**Design.** The construct to model is tdMCP, a fusion linker, and a passenger
protein, with the MS2 operator bound to tdMCP. Joining two proteins directly tends
to distort both, and a linker buys connectivity with spatial separation and
flexibility, but its composition and length also move the passenger, the adaptor and
the RNA relative to each other. So we treated passenger and linker as two variables
and screened them together rather than picking one linker and assuming it
generalised. Eight linker conditions: direct fusion as the explicit baseline, GGGGS
repeated once to four times (5, 10, 15 and 20 aa) as the flexible class, EAAAK once
and twice (5 and 10 aa) as the relatively rigid α-helical class, both standard
fusion-protein linkers (Chen et al. 2013) `[LIT]`, and PAPAP, a short linker of the
same length as one GGGGS but from a different sequence class (Zhao et al. 2008; Liu
et al. 2024) `[LIT]`.

Eleven passengers, chosen for three different reasons. Four are benchmarks with
high-resolution references: MBP, 370 aa, PDB 1ANF, which also has MS2-fusion
precedent in RNA affinity purification (Batey and Kieft 2007) `[LIT]`; sfGFP, 231 aa,
2B3P; mCherry, 220 aa, 2H5Q; and the plant lectin GNA, 109 aa, 1MSA. Four are
structurally characterised bee proteins: AmOBP14, 118 aa, 3S0A; MRJP1, 413 aa, 5YYL; ASP1, 119 aa, 3BJH; OBP5,
121 aa, 3R72. Three have no experimental structure and are in the panel because
proteomics identified them as bee-derived proteins in the guts of feeding _Varroa
destructor_ mites (Piou et al. 2024) `[LIT]`: OBP18, Hexamerin70b and Transferrin.
Those three were detected **after ingestion**, which says nothing about an uptake
mechanism or delivery capability, so we carried them as biologically motivated
candidates and not as proven carriers.

**Build.** 11 passengers × 8 linker conditions × 5 diffusion samples = 440 models,
at the Stage C settings and scored the same way, each against the tdMCP control and,
where one exists, against the passenger's own experimental structure.

**Test.** The pilot asked the two questions separately: does adding the passenger
move the RNA, and does the passenger still look like its reference? With MBP, GGGGS1
gave 4/5 reference-like models with no linker–RNA or MBP–RNA contacts and no severe
clashes of either kind, the cleanest result in the panel. GGGGS4 also gave 4/5, but
one of its five predictions carried 13 linker–RNA contacts, 8 severe linker–RNA
clashes, 3 MBP–RNA contacts and 3 MBP–tdMCP severe clashes, so counting
reference-like models is not on its own enough. Swapping MBP for the bee protein
AmOBP14 and changing nothing else dropped GGGGS1 from 4/5 to **1/5**, while across
all 35 AmOBP14 predictions the passenger fold stayed within 0.620–0.875 Å of 3S0A,
median 0.711 Å, including in models that put the RNA in the wrong place.

**Result: no universal linker, and two findings that outlast the panel.**

| Condition  | Reference-like models / 55 | Improved on direct fusion | Worse than direct fusion |
| ---------- | -------------------------- | ------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| direct     | 29/55                      | —                         | —                        |
| GGGGS1     | 22/55                      | 2/11                      | 7/11                     |
| GGGGS2     | 32/55                      | 5/11                      | 3/11                     |
| GGGGS3     | 28/55                      | 4/11                      | 4/11                     |
| **GGGGS4** | **40/55**                  | **8/11**                  | **1/11**                 |
| EAAAK1     | 29/55                      | 4/11                      | 4/11                     |
| **EAAAK2** | **38/55**                  | **7/11**                  | **1/11**                 |
| PAPAP      | 32/55                      | 4/11                      | 3/11                     |

No linker reached 5/5 for every passenger and no passenger reached 5/5 with every
linker. GNA reaches 5/5 with PAPAP while ASP1 manages 1/5 with the same linker; OBP5
reaches 5/5 with both GGGGS2 and GGGGS4; Transferrin reaches 5/5 with EAAAK2 and 1/5
with GGGGS1 and GGGGS2; sfGFP moves from 1/5 with GGGGS3 to 4/5 with GGGGS4. Nor is
there a length trend: lengthening the flexible linker from 5 to 20 aa does not
progressively improve anything, and PAPAP, the same length as GGGGS1, behaves
differently from it.

The first finding that outlasts the panel is the shape of the whole prediction
landscape. Across 370 models, RNA placement is not a continuum from good to bad but
two states:

| RNA-placement state         | Models  | Median protein-aligned RNA RMSD | Experimental contacts |
| --------------------------- | ------- | ------------------------------- | --------------------- |
| lower-RMSD, contact-keeping | 215/370 | ~1.25 Å                         | median 32/39          |
| alternative                 | 155/370 | ~21.3 Å                         | **0/39 in all 155**   |

Steric interference does not explain the alternative state. Only 5 of the 370
models, about 1.4%, carried more than five combined fusion-specific severe clashes,
and those outliers fall in both states, so the passenger is not simply colliding
with the RNA and pushing it away.

The second finding is that the model's own confidence cannot tell the two states
apart. Median Boltz `confidence_score` was 0.867 for the contact-keeping state and
0.871 for the ~21 Å alternative, so the wrong state scored marginally higher. pTM,
ipTM, protein ipTM, complex pLDDT and the related outputs all overlap heavily, the
largest median difference across the metrics we examined being under 0.03.

Passenger folding and RNA docking also came apart. For the six passengers with
experimental references, 41 of 42 passenger × linker constructs had a lower median
passenger RMSD than their own direct-fusion baseline, the exception being MRJP1 with
GGGGS3, so a linker generally helps the cargo keep its predicted fold. But passenger
RMSD against contact retention gives Pearson r ≈ −0.08, and against RNA-placement
RMSD r ≈ +0.08. Both are negligible: a construct can hold a well-preserved cargo
fold and still put the RNA in the wrong place.

**What we learnt.** Linker behaviour is passenger-dependent, so a passenger–linker
pair is the unit that has to be screened, not a linker on its own. GGGGS4 and EAAAK2
are the sensible first two to try on a new cargo. Preserving the cargo fold does not
license any claim about the RNA interface, and the two have to be checked
separately. And a prediction run without an experimental reference would have been
uninterpretable here, because confidence alone would have ranked the wrong state
first.

Six limitations bound all of it. Boltz predictions are predictions, not structures.
The five diffusion samples per construct are repeat runs of one model, so x/5 is the
spread of five outputs and not a probability or a population of states. OBP18,
Hexamerin70b and Transferrin have no experimental passenger reference and we did not
substitute a predicted one and treat it as experimental. GNA and MRJP1 are
oligomeric in their native assemblies while our comparison scores a single predicted
chain. And the 11-passenger matrix merges two computational batches run at the same
frozen settings, so cross-passenger totals are descriptive rather than one
homogeneous experiment. Structural plausibility is not delivery: none of this shows
that a fusion reaches a mite.

**What it changed.** The adaptor arm now carries a screening rule instead of a
single construct: each candidate passenger goes through the eight linker conditions
with GGGGS4 and EAAAK2 first, scored against both the tdMCP control and the
passenger's own reference. It gave the GFP proof of concept in
[5.4](/engineering#cycle-5-4) its linker shortlist, and the next step is bench
validation of a selected passenger–linker architecture rather than more models.
