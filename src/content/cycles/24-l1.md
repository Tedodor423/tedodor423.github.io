# Iteration 1 — delivering dsRNA to larvae

**Question.** dsRNA has to be fed to larvae before we can ask how stable it is over
capping, the point at which the workers seal the cell over with wax. How much, in
what volume, and can we get the larva back afterwards?

**Design.** In our first iteration we fed 1 µg of dsRNA to individual larvae: 5 µL of
our 200 ng/µL solution of 700 bp GFP dsRNA, from a 20 µL micropipette. The sole
purpose of this pilot was to get used to the mechanics of feeding larvae and
extracting from them.

The route itself was settled first. In-house larval rearing was ruled out as
infeasible in the time available (two to three months of training, and "a paper in
itself" at the lab that does it), so we consulted leading entomologists, including
Prof. Geraldine Wright, and agreed that **in-hive dosing was the best approach given
the timeframe**. The regulations around administering nucleic acid to a hive are on
[safety and security](/project-safety).

**Build.** We took brood frames from the hive and pipetted the dsRNA solution
on-the-spot into the cells of **5th instar larvae**, 10 larvae per test: one group on
the dsRNA solution and one on 5 µL of 1 M sucrose. The frame went straight back into
its hive, and after 24 h we took it out again and practised extracting larval
haemolymph. We chose 5th instar larvae lying in two rows and marked the first larval
position with latitudinal and longitudinal marks in coloured pen on the sides of the
frame, one colour for the start of the 10 test samples and another for the 10
controls.

**Test.** Whether the larva takes the dose, checked 24 h later, and whether
haemolymph can be recovered from it.

**Result.** The **5 µL feeding volume was too low**: it fell as a small droplet onto
the surface of the larva. Nurse bees feed larvae large volumes of nutrient-rich
secretions that cover the larva completely, and the larva consumes them in one go
(Heimken et al., 2009). Our 5 µL droplet neither covered the larva nor could reliably
be placed near its mouth region.

Extracting haemolymph by cutting in at an insertion point and drawing fluid with a
19-gauge needle was also unreliable. Larvae often burst, contaminating the
haemolymph with intestinal fluid.

**What we learnt.** To fix both problems, we would **feed a larger volume and find a
more reliable way to sample the larva**. The sampling problem is
[cycle L2](/engineering#cycle-l2).

**What it changed: a 20 µL dose.** We diluted the 200 ng/µL stock 4× by adding 15 µL
of 1 M sucrose, so each larva received 20 µL carrying the same 1 µg, and repeated the
pilot with 10 larvae on that dose and 10 controls on 20 µL of 1 M sucrose. **The
larvae were fully covered by the solution, and it visibly disappeared.** With our bee
lab mentor's advice we concluded that the larvae had eaten all of it, and that the
dsRNA in it was ingested. The repeat also replaced pen marks with **photographs of
the frame and a coloured digital overlay** marking each sampled cell by group. Since
then every larval experiment on this project has used in-hive dosing of 5th instar
larvae with **20 µL carrying 1 µg**.
