# Does the model tell us anything about the state we are modelling?

**Question.** How does a small initial number of _Varroa_ in a colony grow to a
number large enough to cause colony collapse and significant honey losses, and over
what timescale, given different initial conditions?

**Design.** The purpose of this first cycle was to establish use of the BEEHAVE model
to understand _Varroa_ impacts at the hive level. We also sought to understand the
determinants of _Varroa_ spread through a single hive, and to identify variables that
do and do not affect spread and hence the time to colony collapse. BEEHAVE couples
colony demography, foraging and mite population dynamics in one agent-based model, so
a treatment can be judged **against colony survival** rather than against mite counts.

**Build.** The BEEHAVE model was run using **NetLogo 5.3.1**. The key inputs were the
hive profile, the initial number of _Varroa_, and the percentage carrying deformed
wing virus, which is deadly to bees in the model. The hive profile encompasses the
environment surrounding a hive, the hive's internal characteristics, and beekeeper
interventions such as the addition of pollen and extraction of honey. For this cycle,
**the default BEEHAVE hive profile was used** — no treatment layer.

**Test.** The initial number of _Varroa_ was measured as the **varroa infestation
level (VIL)**, a common metric equal to the number of mites per 100 bees. Initial VIL
was set between 0% and 100% in increments of 20, with deformed wing virus (DWV) held
constant at 50%. The initial percentage of mites with DWV was then set between 0% and
100%, also in increments of 20, with VIL held constant at 20%. Time to colony
collapse and the percentage loss in honey production from year 1 to year 5 (or the
year of collapse) were tracked as the key economic outputs.

**Result.** The tests produced **a roughly straight line**. Regardless of the VIL or
the percentage with DWV, the effect on the hive within 5 years is roughly the same:
the colony still collapses in an average of **4 years**, and honey production is
affected similarly no matter how many _Varroa_ infest the hive.

**What we learnt.** _Varroa_ spread **exhibits limited sensitivity to initial
conditions**. This lent credibility to the idea that, with a single therapy capable
of treating _Varroa_ at the levels found in the model, we could apply it successfully
across a range of initial conditions. If a hive starting with a low infestation of 10
mites can be rescued from collapse, a hive starting with 100 can be too, because
their steady states at 5 years are essentially the same.

**Limitation.** This does not eliminate the possibility that _Varroa_ spread is
sensitive to **hive-related** parameters, such as the number of brood cells. Testing
that requires parametrising the hive according to real-life conditions.
