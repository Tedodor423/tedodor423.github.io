# Iteration 3 — the pollen patty

**Question.** A key limitation of current *Varroa* dsRNA therapeutics is the
delivery method: dsRNA is delivered in a sucrose solution, in which it has a very
short half-life. What do we deliver it in instead?

**Design.** Yeast is often incorporated into a nutrient supplement for honeybees
called a **pollen patty** — sugars, protein supplements such as yeast, and binding
agents. The patty is administered as a slab on the top of the hive, which worker and
nurse bees feed on. An extensive literature review and a discussion with **Professor
Geraldine Wright**, who had engineered yeast to deliver vital sterols in a pollen
patty, gave four advantages of using yeast as both the dsRNA chassis and the
delivery vehicle:

- Engineered yeast can be **heat inactivated** and incorporated into a supplement
  that bees readily feed on.
- Yeast can be significantly enriched in the patty **without affecting
  palatability**, increasing the dsRNA dose administered.
- Heat-inactivated yeast is not considered a GMO `[FLAG]`.
- dsRNA fed to nurse bees is transferred to the mite via the **glandular secretions
  of nurse bees**, which mites feed on `[FLAG]`.

Professor Wright also recommended a pollen patty formulation, which we adjusted to
maximise the dsRNA dose.

**Build.** Not written up.

**Test.** The comparison that decides this cycle is **stability in sucrose against
stability in the pollen patty**. It has not been run.
