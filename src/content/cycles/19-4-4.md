# Does a protein ride the dsRNA across the bee gut?

**Question.** 4.1 and 4.3 both assume a protein can be attached to the dsRNA and
still be there, and still be folded, on the far side of the bee gut. Is that
assumption testable now, without waiting for a Vg binder that does not exist?

**Design.** Replace the unknown cargo with a known one. GFP is a reporter whose
folding _is_ its signal, so a GFP that still fluoresces in haemolymph after being
fed as an RNA-protein complex **answers two questions at once**: the complex
survived the gut, and the protein survived with it. This is the cheap version of
4.3 with the risky half removed.

**Build.** A cassette expressing MS2 coat protein linked to GFP, expressed in
_E. coli_ and purified, assembled onto loop-ended dsRNA carrying an MS2 hairpin
array in one loop. The array geometry is the one 4.1 arrived at.

**Test.** The protein-dsRNA complex was fed to adult bees; haemolymph was extracted
at 1 h and GFP fluorescence read on a plate reader.

**Result.** Not in the written record.

reporter whose signal depends on its own folding is the right cargo for a
first-pass functionalisation test, because it makes "did it survive" and "is it
still working" **the same measurement**.
