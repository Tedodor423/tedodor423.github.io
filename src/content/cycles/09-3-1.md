# Iteration 1 — RNA extraction from honeybees

**Question.** Which extraction method recovers dsRNA from larvae and larval
haemolymph with the least loss and the least variance?

**Design.** rt-qPCR, the gold standard for RNA quantification, requires an RNA
purification step for biological samples with high background noise and
autofluorescence. We wanted to identify the **highest-yield RNA purification protocol
for whole larval homogenates**, and the two methods we considered were a
silica-column-based extraction and a simple phenol/chloroform extraction. The working
principle was as little processing as possible: the dsRNA mass we are chasing is
small and every step loses some. We compared five method variants against each other
rather than adopting a default.

**Build.** Whole larvae were extracted from hives, homogenised and processed under
the two protocols. Five extraction variants were run on 5th-instar larvae fed 1 µg
dsRNA: liquid nitrogen grinding, standard protocol, DTT addition, extended 40 min
Vezol incubation, and spin-down after Vezol addition.

**Test.** Larvae were dosed with **1 µg of dsRNA**, removed from the hive whole after
**24 hours**, flash frozen and stored. Yield and purity by NanoDrop across all five
variants, same larvae, same day (29 Jul).

**Result: a clean null.** **All five methods gave comparable yields.** No method was
better. All five also gave very low 260/230 ratios, traced to residual ethanol and
fixed by using 1× volume ethanol instead of 0.5×, a dry spin after the ethanol has
passed the column, and leaving the lid open until the ethanol has evaporated.

A second, quantitative comparison gave the decision that mattered. Five larvae were
flash-frozen, homogenised and pooled to roughly 100 µL each, then split into
aliquots of about 60 µL, so that biological variation is removed and what is left
is method variation:

| Method                         | Mean concentration (ng/µL) | SD (ng/µL) | CV (%) |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------- | ---------- | ------ |
| Phenol-chloroform              | 1255.15                    | 344.04     | 27.41  |
| Silica beads, **Vazyme** kit   | 261.33                     | 48.24      | 18.46  |

Concentration, A260/280 and A260/230 were read on the NanoDrop for every aliquot,
and the mean, SD and coefficient of variation computed from those reads.

Phenol-chloroform yields roughly 4.8× more total RNA and is half again as variable
(n = 8 against n = 7). TRIzol A260/230 ran 0.51–0.88, which is real guanidine and
phenol carryover and a qPCR inhibition risk.

**What we learnt.** The coefficient of variation of the Vazyme kit using silica beads
was much lower and, **despite the lower yield, we chose it for RNA extraction as it
was more suitable for providing accurate downstream dsRNA quantification with qPCR.**
Column beats TRIzol **on variance, not on yield**, and TRIzol's
variance is structural: five operator-dependent steps stack multiplicatively, and
the manual specifies the aqueous-phase transfer only as "angling the tube at 45°",
with no defined volume. Two people differ by more than 20%. The five-method null
saves a future team a week, so we publish it.

**Limitation.** The comparison was on total RNA yield by NanoDrop, not on
dsRNA-specific recovery. A method could give identical total RNA and different
duplex recovery. Spike-recovery is **the missing control**.
