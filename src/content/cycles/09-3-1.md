# Iteration 1 — processing honeybee samples

**Question.** RT-qPCR is the gold standard for RNA quantification, and bee material
is not a sample it was built for. What has to happen to adult haemolymph, and to a
whole larva, before either can go into a reaction?

**Design.** The two tissues need opposite treatments.

Adult haemolymph is comparatively clear and can be run directly, without an RNA
purification step. Its problem is that it **melanises quickly**, turning darker and
more viscous on exposure to heat and air, which undermines the measurement. Reviewing
the literature on PCR from insect haemolymph pointed at tyrosinase inhibition, so we
took **0.1 mM N-phenylthiourea (PTU) with a five-fold dilution in RNase-free water**
as the treatment to test. The rate at which untreated haemolymph darkens is quantified
on [measurement](/measurement).

Whole larval homogenate is the opposite case: too viscous and too impure to go in
directly, so it needs an extraction step, and we wanted the **highest-yield
protocol** for it. The two candidates were a silica-column extraction and a simple
phenol/chloroform extraction. The working principle was as little processing as
possible — the dsRNA mass we are chasing is small and every step loses some — so we
compared five method variants against each other rather than adopting a default.

**Build.** Untreated adult haemolymph was taken from newly emerged worker bees, flash
frozen and stored; on the day of the experiment samples were either treated with
0.1 mM PTU and diluted five-fold, or left untreated as the comparison.

Whole larvae were extracted from hives, homogenised and processed under the two
protocols. Five extraction variants were run on 5th-instar larvae fed 1 µg dsRNA:
liquid nitrogen grinding, standard protocol, DTT addition, extended 40 min Vezol
incubation, and spin-down after Vezol addition.

**Test.** For the adult arm, PTU plus five-fold dilution against undiluted, untreated
haemolymph, scored on how far the sample darkens. For the larval arm: larvae dosed
with **1 µg of dsRNA**, removed from the hive whole after **24 hours**, flash frozen
and stored, then yield and purity by NanoDrop across all five variants, same larvae,
same day (29 Jul).

**Result: a clean null on the extraction variants.** **All five methods gave
comparable yields.** No method was better. All five also gave very low 260/230 ratios,
traced to residual ethanol and fixed by using 1× volume ethanol instead of 0.5×, a dry
spin after the ethanol has passed the column, and leaving the lid open until the
ethanol has evaporated.

A second, quantitative comparison gave the decision that mattered. Five larvae were
flash-frozen, homogenised and pooled to roughly 100 µL each, then split into
aliquots of about 60 µL, so that biological variation is removed and what is left
is method variation:

| Method                       | Mean concentration (ng/µL) | Mean A260/A280 | SD (ng/µL) | CV (%) |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------- | -------------- | ---------- | ------ |
| Phenol-chloroform            | 1255.15                    | 1.80           | 344.04     | 27.40  |
| Silica beads, **Vazyme** kit | 261.33                     | 2.04           | 48.25      | 18.50  |

Concentration, A260/280 and A260/230 were read on the NanoDrop for every aliquot,
and the mean, SD and coefficient of variation computed from those reads.

Phenol-chloroform yields roughly 4.8× more total RNA and is half again as variable
(n = 8 against n = 7). TRIzol A260/230 ran 0.51–0.88, which is real guanidine and
phenol carryover and a qPCR inhibition risk.

**What we learnt.** Insect haemolymph has to be prepared before it can be amplified,
one way or the other: prevent the extract melanising, or take the RNA out of it. For
larvae that means extraction, and **the coefficient of variation of the Vazyme silica
bead kit was much lower and its protein purity better, so despite the lower yield we
chose it for accurate downstream dsRNA quantification by qPCR.** Column beats TRIzol
**on variance, not on yield**, and TRIzol's variance is structural: five
operator-dependent steps stack multiplicatively, and the manual specifies the
aqueous-phase transfer only as "angling the tube at 45°", with no defined volume. Two
people differ by more than 20%. The five-method null saves a future team a week, so we
publish it.

**Limitation.** The comparison was on total RNA yield by NanoDrop, not on
dsRNA-specific recovery. A method could give identical total RNA and different
duplex recovery. Spike-recovery is **the missing control**.

**What it changed.** Column extraction standardised for larvae, PTU and dilution for
adult haemolymph, and two sample routes fixed for the feeding experiments of
[cycle 3.3](/engineering#cycle-3-3). Both protocols are on
[experiments and lab book](/wet-lab-experiments).
