title: Why RNAi rather than another chemical?
hear: lam*, budge*, le-feuvre, hiatt, us-regulators, colin, mcloughlin
---

## Hear

The costs of chemical acaricides led to our interest in a fundamentally
different mechanism for control: RNA interference, which from our R&D
perspective gave much room for investigating and optimising NECTAR. To confirm
whether we would pursue dsRNA rather than another chemical, we consulted
environmental, beekeeping, laboratory and regulatory perspectives. These
conversations exposed the severe limitations of chemical acaricides, supported
our idea to pursue RNAi-based treatments, and sparked changes to our initial
project design to prioritise effectiveness, scalability and safety.

## Observe

The US EPA has already registered two RNAi-based biopesticides, one of which,
Norroa, controls Varroa populations. It requires weeks to take effect because it
targets mite reproduction instead of survival, and it targets worker bees
instead of the larvae Varroa primarily feeds upon. Realising both the
feasibility of regulatory approval and the potential to benefit beekeepers with
higher treatment efficacy, our focus shifted towards how we could improve on the
existing product to meet stakeholder needs.

## Navigate

Combining the challenges faced by Norroa with stakeholders' concerns about
treatment efficacy, practicality and safety, we adapted our project design. We
chose to target genes that kill mites directly to improve efficacy, proposed two
practical dsRNA delivery systems (inactivated yeast in pollen patties, or an
engineered living gut bacterium) to prioritise scalability, and minimised
off-target effects with bioinformatic tools to promote safety.

## Evaluate

Evaluating dsRNA against current chemical controls, we identified three key
benefits: effectiveness, modularity and biodegradability. Its new mode of action
could kill mites resistant to conventional acaricides and be incorporated into
IPM, its modularity means dsRNA resistance could potentially be managed by
targeting other genes, and it would be degraded in the ecosystem by UV light and
microbial systems, further reducing risks to non-target organisms.

## Yield

We decided to proceed with our RNAi-based approach rather than another chemical,
since it was feasible from regulatory and scientific perspectives, could better
support beekeepers, and was expected to have less environmental impact.
Reflecting on what we heard and observed, we changed the specifics of that
approach, modifying how we selected our gene target to improve efficacy and
safety, and proposing delivery methods with enhanced commercial scalability. As
we added more features, we realised we could develop NECTAR into a platform for
designing RNAi-based treatments.
