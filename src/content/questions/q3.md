## Hear

We began by listening to beekeepers, industry leaders and honey businesses to
understand what a useful Varroa treatment would actually need to achieve. Their
feedback showed that efficacy alone was not enough: cost, labour, frequency of
application, compatibility with existing practices, residues in honey,
resistance and public acceptance all mattered. Some stakeholders valued
*S. alvi* because its persistence could reduce repeated treatment; others
preferred pollen patties and a non-living formulation, which fitted more easily
into existing workflows and avoided concerns around living GMOs.

## Observe

A living engineered *S. alvi* system could trigger additional requirements
around GMO release, containment, spread between colonies and effects on
non-target organisms. A heat-inactivated yeast product could avoid some of these
concerns while still requiring evidence for dsRNA safety, environmental fate and
possible residues in honey. International differences in regulation, organic
certification and consumer expectations showed that deployment depended not only
on biological performance, but on where and how the product would be used.

## Navigate

We changed the product itself. Rather than relying on a living bacterium to
persist inside bees, we shifted towards engineered *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* to
produce dsRNA, followed by heat inactivation and formulation into a non-living
product. Yeast offered mature fermentation infrastructure, established
engineering tools, favourable dsRNA accumulation and the possibility of avoiding
expensive purification. To compensate for the loss of biological persistence, we
designed the treatment around pollen patties already used by beekeepers.

## Evaluate

Heat inactivation provides biocontainment by preventing the engineered yeast
from reproducing or establishing itself in the hive, reducing the concerns that
come with a persistent living chassis. The dsRNA remains biologically active,
however, so off-target effects, environmental degradation, waste management and
residue risks still need to be assessed. We also considered scalability and
responsible innovation, with industrial yeast infrastructure supporting a more
practical route from manufacture to deployment.

## Yield

We moved from a living, self-replicating *S. alvi* system towards a
yeast-produced, non-living dsRNA formulation delivered through an established
hive-feeding method. This keeps the advantages of synthetic biology for
controlled dsRNA production while avoiding the need for an engineered organism
to persist in the environment. Our final design therefore reflects not just what
could work biologically, but what could be contained, regulated, manufactured,
accepted and realistically used by beekeepers.
