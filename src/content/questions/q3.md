title: Should we be making a living GMO at all?
hear: le-feuvre*, allerton, comvita, sandham, morrison, scottish-government, mcloughlin
observe: ogtr*, dunwell*, krebs, hartley, bowden, us-regulators
evaluate: barrick*, coy
---

## Hear

Beekeepers did not line up behind us. Allerton was drawn to the living
*S. alvi* route precisely because it cut labour, the biggest problem with
any treatment; Le Feuvre called persistence in the hive attractive before
listing what it would need — public acceptance, containment, communication,
regulation. The patty answer to the labour problem came from beekeepers who
already feed patties.

## Observe

Four jurisdictions, one boundary. The OGTR was binary: the non-living yeast
product sits outside its remit, both living *S. alvi* versions inside, and
an inducible switch changes the risk assessment but not the route. Dunwell
refused the easy conclusion — assessment follows pathways to harm, whatever
made the dsRNA. Bowden placed heat-killed yeast in a grey area; the US
agencies said living versus non-living changes what evidence is required,
not how hard the path is. Hartley added that a GMO anywhere in the
production pathway can cost a product its organic status.

## Navigate

The criteria widened from efficacy to deployability: containment,
regulation, food safety, beekeeper labour, manufacturing cost, social
licence. The decision was to separate the place where the GMO produces
NECTAR from the place where NECTAR is used: engineered yeast in a fermenter,
heat inactivation, and a product that cannot replicate in the hive.

## Evaluate

Heat inactivation is biocontainment by design, which matters most against
Barrick's warning — from having engineered *S. alvi* himself — that it can
persist in wild bee species. Killing the organism does not kill the RNA, so
the environmental question moves to the molecule. Coy's responsible-innovation
lens backed the choice on trust, affordability and scalability.

## Yield

We stopped building the inducible *S. alvi* and moved to engineered yeast,
heat-inactivated before it reaches a colony. The dissent stands: Dunwell
holds that the delivery method is secondary to the endpoint, and we say so
rather than smoothing it over. Next question: we removed the organism, not
the RNA — what can the RNA still harm?
