title: How and why is Varroa a problem, and what treatments exist?
hear: le-feuvre*, ford, allerton, scottish-government, budge, sandham, oxnatbees
observe: frost, hiatt
---

## Hear

*Varroa destructor* feeds on adult and larval bees and vectors deformed wing
virus and the acute bee paralysis virus complex; untreated colonies mostly
collapse within two to three years. Le Feuvre speaks for the Australian industry: a
A$100 million eradication programme failed, the mite is endemic, and
beekeepers are leaving over profitability and Varroa control. Ford reports the
regional split — New South Wales beekeepers down more than 1,200 hives and about
half their honey production. Allerton calls the impact devastating: Amateur
Beekeepers Australia went from 5,000 members to 3,000 when Varroa arrived, and is
back to 4,000. The
Scottish team rank it in their top three threats, which makes it a problem beyond
our two case studies. Sandham and the Oxfordshire group dissent: treating at all
may be the wrong reflex.

## Observe

Resistance is present tense. Frost reports dual resistance already detected
in Australia; Hiatt, farming 18,000 colonies in the US, went from one CheckMite+
strip a year to 55% losses when the mites caught up, and rotation still costs
50–60% in bad years. What
remains has hard limits: formic acid's temperature ceiling, no brood break
across most of Australia, thyme oil tainting honey, and off-label homemade
treatments filling the gap. The literature says the same by chemical and
date: pyrethroid resistance from Italy in 1991, amitraz from the USA in 1998,
coumaphos from Italy in 2001, resistance to several treatments at once, and
coumaphos accumulating in brood and beeswax.

## Navigate

What would help is a treatment that outlasts evolving resistance, keeps
residues out of honey, beeswax and brood, and cuts the labour the current
methods demand. The problem stopped being mite mortality and became a system:
colony survival, repeated labour, rebuilding costs and the pollination on top
of all of it. The treatment-free beekeepers narrowed the audience: small varied
apiaries can adapt, large uniform commercial operations cannot. Our target
user became the commercial beekeeper.

## Evaluate

A single mode of action is a countdown. Whatever we built had to be designed
for resistance from the start, and had to hold for months rather than weeks,
because a treatment that needs repeating every few weeks is the labour
problem beekeepers were already leaving over.

## Yield

Multiple RNA targets and resistance management in the RNA design, and a
duration target the bee-lab persistence work is measured against. Next
question: if rotating four chemicals still loses half the colonies, is
another chemical the answer at all?
