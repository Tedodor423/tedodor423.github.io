## Hear

Ford put numbers on the burden — roughly 60% product and 40% labour, formic
acid at about A$7 a hive compounding over a thousand hives — and an order of
priorities: cost, then effectiveness, then time and labour. Goodrich
modelled the year: treatment products alone are 9% of costs before the
labour to apply them, with one worker to about 700 colonies. Hiatt wants
months of protection and no extra trip to 18,000 hives.

## Observe

In our own dataset, NSW beekeepers report about A$22.18 per hive of direct
Varroa control plus 0.37 hours of labour per hive, and US Varroa control
runs to about US$9.22 per colony a year `[CALC]`.

## Navigate

Thurman set the method: the counterfactual is the best existing treatment,
not doing nothing; operation level first, then a simple partial-equilibrium
model of paid pollination — and never multiply an industry's value by a
treatment's efficacy. The delivery answer fell out of the same
conversations: the treatment goes into the patty the beekeeper is already
putting in the hive.

## Evaluate

Under current assumptions NECTAR returns a net private benefit per treated
colony in both case-study countries `[CALC]`. The unsolved tension is
Frost's: commercial beekeepers used to synthetics driving counts to
undetectable may distrust a product that leaves a detectable alcohol-wash
count.

## Yield

Pollen-patty delivery, chosen by stakeholders rather than by the lab, and an
economic model turned inside out: start from what a beekeeper can afford and
work back to the yeast titre the fermentation has to reach. Next question:
every number above came from Australia or California — does any of it hold
anywhere else?
