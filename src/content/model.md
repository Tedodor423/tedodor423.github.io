This is a hub, not a dumping ground for graphs. Each model gets its own page and
its own argument; this page explains how they fit together and what each was
for.

## An honest statement, up front

All of our models are parameterised from the literature. No model currently uses
a parameter we measured ourselves. That is a real limitation and it is stated
here, at the top, rather than left for a judge to discover three pages in.

It also defines the most valuable thing the next iteration of this project could
do: close the loop between bench and model.

## How every model on this wiki is written

The same eight beats, every time:

**Question → Inputs → Assumptions → Model → Sensitivity and validation →
Prediction → Decision it informed → Limitations**

And every model carries one visible box:

> **How did this change NECTAR?**
>
> If the honest answer is "it didn't", the model does not deserve the space.
> Cut it, or move it to a supplementary section and say why it is there.

Modelled outputs are marked 🔵 throughout the wiki. They are predictions, never
results.

## The models

### RNA design

Choosing the sequence is itself a modelling problem, accessibility,
thermodynamics and off-target risk, combined into a ranking. See
[RNA design](/software).

### Economic modelling

Turns an affordable price into a manufacturing cost and then into a required
production titre, a model whose output is an engineering specification. See
[economic modelling](/economic-modelling).

### Ecological modelling

Colony and mite population dynamics, and what a treatment would have to achieve
to matter at colony scale. See [ecological modelling](/ecological-modelling).

## How the models connect

The useful story is the chain, not the individual models: what a beekeeper can
afford constrains manufacturing cost, which constrains required titre, which
becomes a design requirement for [yeast](/yeast), while the ecological model
sets what efficacy has to reach for any of it to be worth doing.
