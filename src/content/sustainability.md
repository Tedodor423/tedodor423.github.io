Sustainable development means meeting present needs without compromising the
ability of future generations to meet theirs. Asserting relevance to a goal is
easy and worth nothing. This page does the harder thing: it breaks each goal
into specific problems, says what our work does about each, and says where our
work makes something else worse.

This page only works because the stakeholder engagement behind it is real. It is
not a standalone exercise, and it should read as continuous with
[human practices](/human-practices).

## How each goal is treated

Five blocks, the same five for every goal:

1. **The goal and its targets**: the actual target text, by number.
2. **Problem identification**: two or three discrete, concrete problems. Not a
   general claim about importance.
3. **Positive long-term impacts**: mapped one-to-one onto those problems.
4. **Negative interactions**: what this could make worse, including in other
   goals, cited by target number.
5. **Stakeholder input**: a named person who works on this, and what they said.

Block four is the one that distinguishes a real analysis from a list. Negative
interactions are the scoring mechanism, not a disclaimer.

## Negative interactions and tensions

The section that earns this page its marks. Written as questions with honest
answers:

- Can this be accessible regardless of where someone is or what they can afford?
- What waste does it create, and what happens to unused product?
- Could it cause ecological harm while trying to prevent it?
- Does protecting a managed pollinator have costs for wild ones?

Each answer states what we would do about it, and which of those things we have
actually done.
