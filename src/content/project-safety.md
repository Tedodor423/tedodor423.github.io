Safety drove the chassis decision on this project, and put off-target screening
inside the design pipeline rather than after it. That makes this page a record
of engineering choices.

## Risks specific to this system

Generic biosafety text scores nothing. This section is about _our_ system: an
engineered organism producing a double-stranded RNA, inactivated, formulated
into feed, and given to a managed pollinator that forages in the open.

One subsection per risk, each with what could happen, how plausible it is, and
what we did about it.

### Risk to the bees

The host is the delivery vehicle and must not be harmed by it.

### Risk to non-target organisms

Related mites, other arthropods, and anything else exposed through the colony or
through the environment. Sequence-level screening is the first line here; see
[RNA design](/software).

### Risk to people

Handling during production and application, and anything entering the human food
chain through the hive.

### Environmental fate

What happens to the material that is not consumed: persistence, degradation and
disposal.

### Laboratory safety

Containment, waste handling and the local rules the work was done under.

## Why inactivated, and what that argument does not cover

Heat-inactivated yeast is not a living modified organism, and that changes the
regulatory position substantially. It is a genuine safety property and a genuine
design decision.

It is not a complete answer. The material still contains biologically active
RNA; some jurisdictions treat it as an open question; and at least one expert we
spoke to argued that the endpoint matters more than the delivery method. Make
the argument, then state its limits in the same section rather than on a
different page.
