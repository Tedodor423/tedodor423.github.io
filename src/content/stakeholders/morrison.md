## Why we interviewed

We spoke with Michael to understand NECTAR from a social-science and public-acceptance perspective, particularly how we could responsibly investigate attitudes towards dsRNA, GM technologies and our different delivery systems when relatively little social-science research exists specifically around Varroa RNAi.

## What we learned

Michael suggested drawing cautiously on research into technologies such as gene drives and mosquito control, while recognising that different organisms have different public “profiles” and therefore provoke different responses. He also **challenged our assumption that attitudes towards GM technologies would necessarily be highly polarised**. Rather than treating “GMO acceptance” as a simple yes/no question, he encouraged us to investigate responses to the specific ways biotechnology would be used. He also emphasised informed consent, dsRNA-specific regulation and clear communication, suggesting a public-facing glossary to make the technology more accessible.

## How we implemented the advice to change NECTAR

**Michael's feedback directly changed our beekeeper survey design.** Instead of simply asking whether respondents accepted GMOs, we separated our different approaches—including dsRNA-containing pollen patties, constitutively active engineered bacteria and inducible engineered bacteria—to capture more nuanced attitudes. We also placed greater emphasis on dsRNA-specific regulation and incorporated clearer explanations of technical terminology into our communication plans, including developing a glossary for the Wiki.
