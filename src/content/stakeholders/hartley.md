## Why we interviewed

We spoke with Tom to understand whether our different delivery systems could be compatible with organic certification, and whether using NECTAR could affect the organic status of a beekeeper's honey.

## What we learned

Tom explained that **organic certification considers the whole production pathway**, not simply whether the final product contains a living GMO. GMOs are fundamentally incompatible with organic standards, meaning our *S. alvi* approach could create significant barriers. He considered our heat-inactivated yeast approach potentially more feasible, although this would depend on its eventual production and classification. Crucially, we learned that **regulatory approval does not necessarily mean organic acceptability**.

## How we implemented the advice to change NECTAR

His feedback **strengthened our decision to prioritise a non-living, heat-inactivated yeast formulation** and encouraged us to consider certification and market access alongside conventional regulation. However, we retained organic compatibility as an uncertainty rather than assuming that removing the living GMO automatically solved the issue.
