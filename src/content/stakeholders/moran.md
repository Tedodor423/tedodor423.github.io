## Why we interviewed

As the senior author of the 2020 *Science* paper “Engineered symbionts activate honey bee immunity and limit pathogens,” we reached out to Professor Moran to **further explore the regulatory landscape around** *S. alvi* and **seek advice on our bee lab experiments**.

Additionally Professor Moran had recently published a Nature paper on a proposed mechanism of dsRNA uptake in honeybees, so we wanted to discuss any possible **strategies for targeted dsRNA uptake in honeybees**.

## What we learned

Professor Moran highlighted that **a more regulatorily feasible approach** could be the use of *S. alvi* **as a probiotic instead of an engineered gut symbiont**. Professor Moran highlighted a body of work showing that *S. alvi* supplementation in honeybees increased resilience to a variety of pathogens and did not face the same regulatory hurdles as an engineered strain.

When discussing our experiments to investigate the targeted uptake of dsRNA in honeybees, Professor Moran suggested **exploring non-lytic vesicles to drive dsRNA uptake across the honeybee gut**. [Previous work](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-73834-2) has shown that membrane-derived vesicles of *S. alvi* can be enriched in nucleic acids and represent a plausible route of dsRNA delivery from symbiont to host. While beyond the scope of our iGEM project, targeted vesicle-mediated uptake could be an interesting strategy to explore in the future.
