A laboratory result is not a product, and iGEM is not the end of a project that
would need years to reach a hive. This page is written as **a plan, not a promise**.

## What remains unsolved

One problem dominates, and it is worth naming before anything else.

**Yeast titre.** We do not know how much intact, active dsRNA a gram of dry
engineered yeast carries. **That single unmeasured number** is load-bearing in three
places: it decides the manufacturing cost, and therefore whether the product is
affordable at all ([economic modelling](/economic-modelling)); it decides the
dsRNA concentration in the patty, which is one of the two levers the ecological
model is built to compare ([ecological modelling](/ecological-modelling)); and it
is the specification the wet lab would be working to
([yeast](/wet-lab-experiments#yeast-production)). Everything commercial on this wiki is a `[CALC]` that rests on
an assumption about it.

The others, in order of how much they block:

- **Efficacy against mites.** The mite work the project did not reach. Until it
  exists, the ecological model's efficacy threshold has nothing to compare
  against, and the contingent fourth BEEHAVE cycle cannot sensibly be run.
- **dsRNA integrity through the process.** Heat inactivation, drying,
  formulation, storage. Each step is a chance to lose the molecule the product
  is made of.
- **Environmental fate.** The degradation rate of dsRNA in the patty, which the
  safety and sustainability arguments both currently **assume rather than measure**.

## What we would do next

Three things, in this order, because each unblocks the one after it.

1. **Scale production** — measure the titre, then raise it. Nothing downstream
   resolves until this number exists.
2. **Validate efficacy** — the mite assays the project did not reach, against the
   threshold [ecological modelling](/ecological-modelling) sets.
3. **Optimise targets** — more candidates, and more than one at a time, once
   there is a working assay to rank them with.

A fourth is a design ambition rather than a next step: a **multimeric
therapeutic** hitting several targets at once, as resistance management.

## What would have to be true

The assumptions the whole idea rests on.

| Assumption                                                          | Standing        |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------- |
| Heat-killed yeast is not a living modified organism in most regimes | **Established** — confirmed by the CBD Secretariat, with grey areas remaining |
| Pollen patties are a delivery format beekeepers will accept         | **Established** — from beekeeper interviews, not assumed                      |
| Sequence-specific design can avoid off-target silencing             | **Uncertain** — screening is designed, its limits are not fully characterised |
| A bee that eats the patty transfers enough dsRNA to a mite to kill it | **Untested** — the whole of the transfer chain                              |
| Yeast can be made to express dsRNA at a commercially useful titre   | **Untested** — the load-bearing assumption                                    |
| dsRNA survives inactivation, drying and storage                     | **Untested**                                                                  |

Two of the three untested rows are **the same problem** seen from different ends.

## The route beyond the lab

The staged roadmap — laboratory proof, apiary trial, scale-up, regulatory
submission, commercial deployment — is on
[entrepreneurship](/entrepreneurship), along with what each stage needs and the
capabilities we do not have. We are **inside stage one**.

One item on that route has a date attached rather than a dependency: the European
Medicines Agency consultation on RNAi and antisense veterinary medicines closes
**31 October 2026**, and a response is something we could file now rather than
after the science is finished.
