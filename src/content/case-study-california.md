One of two regional studies, following the shape set out on
[the case studies page](/case-studies).

California is the scale case. Migratory commercial beekeeping tied to a
pollination market changes almost every assumption a treatment designed for a
small apiary would make, about labour, timing, transport and what a colony is
worth.

## Hear: the situation on the ground

Losses, treatment rotation and resistance, as described by people operating at
commercial scale.

## Observe: practice, cost, regulation, attitudes

Migratory operations, the pollination contract economics that sit underneath
them, the federal regulatory picture, and attitudes to an engineered input.

## Navigate: what an acceptable treatment would need to do

At this scale, anything requiring per-colony attention is a different product
from anything that can be applied in bulk. Duration, labour and cost per colony
are the binding constraints.

## Evaluate: what this means for NECTAR

Whether the delivery format survives contact with an operation running thousands
of colonies on the road, and where it does not.

## Yield: what changed, and what is still open

Design consequences with links, then the unresolved questions.

## Scale as its own problem

Worth a short section. A treatment that works per colony and a treatment that
works across a migratory operation are not the same product, and the difference
is mostly logistics rather than biology. That is an honest limit on what a
laboratory project can claim, and saying so is stronger than not.
